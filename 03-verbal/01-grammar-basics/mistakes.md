# ⚠️ Grammar Basics: Common Mistakes

> The 10 mistakes students make most often in this topic, why they happen, and how to avoid them.

---

## Top 10 Mistakes

### 1. Matching the verb to the wrong noun
- ❌ *The quality of the apples **are** poor.*
- ✅ *The quality of the apples **is** poor.*
- **Why it happens:** The noun just before the verb (*apples*) is plural, so it feels like the subject.
- **Fix:** Cross out the *of…* phrase. What remains is the real subject.

### 2. Treating "each", "every" and "neither of" as plural
- ❌ *Each of the boys **were** given a prize.*
- ✅ *Each of the boys **was** given a prize.*
- **Why:** The plural noun after *of* distracts you.
- **Fix:** Memorise the list: each, every, either, neither, one of, everyone → always singular.

### 3. Confusing "a number of" and "the number of"
- ❌ *The number of students **are** increasing.*
- ✅ *The number of students **is** increasing.*
- **Why:** Both start with *number*, so they look alike.
- **Fix:** *A* number = many = plural. *The* number = one count = singular.

### 4. Using "will" inside an if/when clause
- ❌ *I will call you when I **will** reach.*
- ✅ *I will call you when I **reach**.*
- **Why:** The meaning is future, so students put *will* everywhere.
- **Fix:** After *if, when, before, after, as soon as, unless*, use the present tense.

### 5. Using the simple past with "since", "already" or "yet"
- ❌ *She **lived** here since 2019.*
- ✅ *She **has lived** here since 2019.*
- **Why:** The action started in the past, so the past tense feels natural.
- **Fix:** Learn the signals: since, for, already, yet, just, ever, never, so far → present perfect.

### 6. Choosing "a/an" by spelling instead of sound
- ❌ *He is **a** honest man. / She joined **an** university.*
- ✅ *He is **an** honest man. / She joined **a** university.*
- **Why:** Students check the first letter, not the pronunciation.
- **Fix:** Say the word aloud. Vowel sound → *an*. Consonant sound → *a*.

### 7. Wrong preposition with a fixed phrase
- ❌ *He is senior than me. / She is good in maths. / We discussed about the plan.*
- ✅ *He is senior **to** me. / She is good **at** maths. / We **discussed** the plan.*
- **Why:** Preposition use is fixed and has no logic to deduce.
- **Fix:** Keep a running list of pairs (see `formulas.md`) and revise it every week.

### 8. Saying "you and I" after a preposition
- ❌ *This is between you and **I**.*
- ✅ *This is between you and **me**.*
- **Why:** "I" sounds more formal and polite.
- **Fix:** Remove the other person. You would say "between me", never "between I".

### 9. Dangling modifiers
- ❌ *Walking down the road, a dog bit me.*
- ✅ *While I was walking down the road, a dog bit me.*
- **Why:** The sentence "sounds fine" when read quickly.
- **Fix:** Ask who is doing the action in the opening phrase. It must match the subject after the comma.

### 10. Missing the "No error" option
- **Mistake:** Always assuming there must be an error in error-spotting questions.
- **Why:** Students feel the question would be pointless otherwise.
- **Fix:** Check each of the 6 rules from the checklist. If none are broken, mark "No error".

---

## 🧠 Pattern Behind Most Mistakes

| Cause | Share of mistakes | Cure |
|---|---|---|
| Not finding the real subject | High | Strike out phrases between subject and verb |
| Ignoring tense signal words | High | Underline *since / for / already / by the time* before choosing |
| Memory gaps in prepositions | Medium | Weekly revision of fixed pairs |
| Rushing | Medium | Read the full sentence once before marking |
