## 🎯 How to Use

Don't copy the entire question every time.

Record:

```text
What I got wrong
        ↓
Why I got it wrong
        ↓
Correct rule
        ↓
One correct example
        ↓
One trap example
```

The goal is to identify **patterns in your mistakes**.

If you repeatedly make the same mistake, that rule becomes a revision priority.

# 🏷️ Mistake Categories

Use one of these tags for every mistake.

| Tag | Meaning         | Example                              |
| --- | --------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `C` | Concept mistake | Didn't know the rule                 |
| `S` | Silly mistake   | Knew the rule but clicked wrong      |
| `T` | Time mistake    | Rushed because of time               |
| `R` | Reading mistake | Missed an important word             |
| `G` | Grammar mistake | Applied the grammar rule incorrectly |

### Example

```text
Mistake Tag: G
Topic: Subject–Verb Agreement
Error: "The list of students are..."
Correct: "The list of students is..."
Reason: Matched the verb with "students" instead of "list".
```

# ⚠️ Common Grammar Traps

These are high-value areas to watch during practice.

## 1. Subject–Verb Agreement

### Trap

> The list of students **are** ready. ❌

### Correct

> The list of students **is** ready. ✅

The subject is **list**, not students.

### Remember

> Ignore the `of + noun` phrase when finding the main subject.

---

## 2. A Number vs The Number

### Trap

> A number of students **is** absent. ❌

### Correct

> A number of students **are** absent. ✅

But:

> The number of students **is** increasing. ✅

### Remember

```text
A number of → plural
The number of → singular
```

---

## 3. Each / Every

### Trap

> Every student **have** an ID card. ❌

### Correct

> Every student **has** an ID card. ✅

### Remember

```text
Each → singular
Every → singular
Everyone → singular
Someone → singular
Nobody → singular
```

---

## 4. Neither...Nor / Either...Or

### Trap

> Neither the manager nor the employees **was** present. ❌

### Correct

> Neither the manager nor the employees **were** present. ✅

The verb agrees with the **nearer subject**.

```text
Neither A nor B → verb follows B
Either A or B   → verb follows B
```

---

## 5. Since vs For

### Trap

> I have lived here **since five years**. ❌

### Correct

> I have lived here **for five years**. ✅

```text
Since → point in time
For   → duration
```

Examples:

> since 2022
> since Monday
> for five years
> for two months

---

## 6. A vs An

### Trap

> He is **a honest** man. ❌

### Correct

> He is **an honest** man. ✅

The choice depends on the **sound**, not simply the first letter.

```text
an honest man
a university
a European
an MBA
```

---

## 7. Information / Advice / Equipment

These are generally treated as **uncountable nouns**.

### Trap

> She gave me some useful **advices**. ❌

### Correct

> She gave me some useful **advice**. ✅

Similarly:

```text
information   ❌ informations
advice        ❌ advices
equipment     ❌ equipments
furniture     ❌ furnitures
```

---

## 8. One of + Plural Noun

### Trap

> He is one of the best **programmer** in the class. ❌

### Correct

> He is one of the best **programmers** in the class. ✅

### Remember

```text
one of the + plural noun
```

---

## 9. Double Comparatives

### Trap

> This method is **more better** than that one. ❌

### Correct

> This method is **better** than that one. ✅

Don't combine:

```text
more + better ❌
more + faster ❌
more + easier ❌
```

---

## 10. Its vs It's

```text
its   → possession
it's  → it is / it has
```

Example:

> The company changed **its** policy. ✅

> **It's** raining outside. ✅

---

## 11. Your vs You're

```text
your  → possession
you're → you are
```

Example:

> Is this **your** laptop?

> **You're** doing well.

---

## 12. Their / There / They're

```text
their  → possession
there  → place/existence
they're → they are
```

Example:

> Their project is impressive.

> The files are over there.

> They're working on the project.

---

## 13. Affect vs Effect

```text
affect → usually a verb
effect → usually a noun
```

Example:

> The change will **affect** the result.

> The change had a major **effect** on performance.

---

## 14. Adjective vs Adverb

### Trap

> She completed the task **quick**. ❌

### Correct

> She completed the task **quickly**. ✅

Remember:

```text
Adjective → describes a noun
Adverb    → describes a verb/adjective/adverb
```

---

## 15. Parallelism

### Trap

> She likes reading, writing, and **to travel**. ❌

### Correct

> She likes reading, writing, and **travelling**. ✅

Keep the structure consistent:

```text
reading
writing
travelling
```

## 🔁 Repeated Mistake Rule

If you make the **same type of mistake three times**, don't just solve more questions.

Do this:

```text
3rd repeated mistake
        ↓
Read the rule again
        ↓
Write 2 correct examples
        ↓
Write 1 trap example
        ↓
Solve 5 similar questions
        ↓
Re-test after 2–3 days
```

---

# ⏱️ Speed Review

When reviewing an error-spotting question, use this order:

```text
1. Subject–Verb Agreement
          ↓
2. Tense
          ↓
3. Pronoun
          ↓
4. Article
          ↓
5. Preposition
          ↓
6. Modifier
          ↓
7. Parallelism
          ↓
8. Word Usage
```

This prevents you from checking every word randomly.

# ✅ Quick Revision Checklist

Before submitting a grammar answer, ask:

* [ ] Does the verb match the real subject?
* [ ] Is the tense correct?
* [ ] Is the article correct?
* [ ] Is the preposition correct?
* [ ] Does the pronoun refer clearly to the right noun?
* [ ] Is the adjective/adverb form correct?
* [ ] Is the comparison grammatically correct?
* [ ] Are list items parallel?
* [ ] Is the word being used correctly?
* [ ] Did I read the complete sentence?

---

## 🧠 Final Rule

> **Don't try to remember every wrong question. Remember the rule that caused the mistake.**

A good mistake log should become **shorter and more valuable over time**, not a huge collection of copied questions.
