# 1. Parts of Speech

Parts of speech classify words according to the role they perform in a sentence.

There are **8 major parts of speech**:

| Part         | Purpose                          | Example          |
| ------------ | -------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Noun         | Names a person/place/thing/idea  | `student`        |
| Pronoun      | Replaces a noun                  | `he`             |
| Verb         | Shows action/state               | `runs`           |
| Adjective    | Describes a noun/pronoun         | `smart`          |
| Adverb       | Modifies a verb/adjective/adverb | `quickly`        |
| Preposition  | Shows relationship               | `in`, `on`, `at` |
| Conjunction  | Connects words/clauses           | `and`, `but`     |
| Interjection | Expresses emotion                | `Wow!`           |

---

## Noun

A **noun** names a person, place, thing, or idea.

```text
Person  → engineer
Place   → Lucknow
Thing   → laptop
Idea    → honesty
```

### Types

* Proper noun → India, Divyansh
* Common noun → country, student
* Collective noun → team, committee
* Abstract noun → honesty, freedom
* Material noun → gold, water

### Common Trap

A noun can be countable or uncountable.

```text
Correct:
many students
much water

Incorrect:
many water
much students
```

---

## Pronoun

A **pronoun** replaces a noun.

```text
Divyansh is preparing for placements.
He practices DSA daily.
```

`He` replaces `Divyansh`.

Common pronouns:

```text
I, we, you, he, she, it, they
me, us, him, her, them
my, our, his, her, their
myself, ourselves, himself, herself
```

---

## Verb

A **verb** expresses an action, occurrence, or state.

```text
She writes code.
He is tired.
They completed the project.
```

### Main types

**Action verb**

```text
run, write, study, build
```

**Helping verb**

```text
is, am, are, was, were
has, have, had
do, does, did
will, shall
```

**Modal verbs**

```text
can, could, may, might, must,
shall, should, will, would
```

---

## Adjective

An adjective describes a noun or pronoun.

```text
a smart student
a difficult question
an efficient algorithm
```

It answers questions such as:

```text
What kind?
Which one?
How many?
```

---

## Adverb

An adverb modifies:

* a verb
* an adjective
* another adverb

Examples:

```text
He runs quickly.
She is extremely intelligent.
He solved the problem very quickly.
```

Many adverbs end in `-ly`, but not all.

```text
quickly
carefully
slowly
```

But:

```text
fast
hard
well
```

can also function as adverbs.

---

## Preposition

A preposition shows a relationship between words.

Common prepositions:

```text
in, on, at, by, with, from, to,
for, of, about, between, among,
under, over, through, during
```

Example:

```text
The book is on the table.
He lives in Lucknow.
She arrived at 5 PM.
```

---

## Conjunction

A conjunction connects words, phrases, or clauses.

### Coordinating conjunctions

Remember:

**FANBOYS**

```text
For
And
Nor
But
Or
Yet
So
```

Example:

```text
I studied hard, but I missed the test.
```

### Subordinating conjunctions

```text
because
although
if
unless
while
when
before
after
since
```

Example:

```text
I stayed home because it was raining.
```

---

## Interjection

An interjection expresses sudden emotion.

```text
Wow!
Oh!
Alas!
Hurrah!
Oops!
```

Usually followed by `!`.

# 2. Subject–Verb Agreement

> **The verb must agree with its subject in number and person.**

### Basic Rule

**Singular subject → singular verb**

```text
He plays cricket.
She works hard.
The student is ready.
```

**Plural subject → plural verb**

```text
They play cricket.
Students work hard.
The students are ready.
```

---

## Rule 1: Ignore words between subject and verb

```text
The quality of these products is good.
```

The subject is `quality`, not `products`.

Therefore:

```text
quality → singular
is → singular
```

### Trap

```text
The quality of these products are good. ❌

The quality of these products is good. ✅
```

---

## Rule 2: Each / Every

`Each` and `every` generally take a **singular noun and singular verb**.

```text
Each student has a laptop. ✅
Every student is present. ✅
```

Not:

```text
Each student have a laptop. ❌
Every student are present. ❌
```

---

## Rule 3: Either / Neither

When used individually:

```text
Either answer is correct.
Neither answer is correct.
```

Usually use a singular verb when the construction is followed by a singular noun.

---

## Rule 4: Either...or / Neither...nor

The verb generally agrees with the **subject closer to it**.

```text
Neither the teacher nor the students are ready.
```

Closest subject:

```text
students → plural
```

Therefore:

```text
are
```

Another example:

```text
Neither the students nor the teacher is ready.
```

Closest subject:

```text
teacher → singular
```

Therefore:

```text
is
```

---

## Rule 5: As well as / Along with / Together with

These expressions do **not** make the subject plural.

```text
The teacher, along with the students, is present.
```

Main subject:

```text
teacher
```

Therefore:

```text
is
```

### Trap

```text
The manager along with his employees are attending. ❌

The manager along with his employees is attending. ✅
```

---

## Rule 6: Collective Nouns

Words such as:

```text
team
family
committee
government
class
```

can behave as singular units depending on context.

```text
The team is winning.
```

Think of the team as one unit.

---

## Rule 7: Uncountable nouns

Some nouns generally take singular verbs.

```text
Information is useful.
Advice is important.
Furniture is expensive.
News is interesting.
```

### Important

```text
The news is good. ✅

The news are good. ❌
```

---

## Rule 8: Mathematics / Physics / Economics

Some subjects ending in `-s` can take a singular verb when referring to the academic subject.

```text
Mathematics is difficult.
Physics is interesting.
Economics is useful.
```

# 3. Tenses

Tense indicates the time of an action or state.

There are three primary time categories:

```text
Present
Past
Future
```

Each can have:

```text
Simple
Continuous
Perfect
Perfect Continuous
```

---

## Present Simple

### Structure

```text
Subject + V1 / V1+s/es
```

Examples:

```text
I study every day.
He studies every day.
```

### Uses

* habits
* routines
* general truths
* facts

```text
The sun rises in the east.
He goes to college every day.
```

---

## Present Continuous

### Structure

```text
Subject + is/am/are + V-ing
```

Examples:

```text
I am studying.
He is working.
They are playing.
```

### Use

Action happening around the present time.

---

## Present Perfect

### Structure

```text
Subject + has/have + V3
```

Examples:

```text
I have completed the assignment.
She has finished the work.
```

### Important

Do not normally use present perfect with a finished specific past-time expression.

```text
I completed the work yesterday. ✅
I have completed the work yesterday. ❌
```

---

## Present Perfect Continuous

### Structure

```text
Subject + has/have been + V-ing
```

Example:

```text
I have been studying for two hours.
```

Often used with:

```text
since
for
```

---

## Past Simple

### Structure

```text
Subject + V2
```

Example:

```text
I studied yesterday.
She went to college.
```

Common time indicators:

```text
yesterday
last week
last year
ago
in 2024
```

---

## Past Continuous

### Structure

```text
Subject + was/were + V-ing
```

Example:

```text
I was studying.
They were playing.
```

---

## Past Perfect

### Structure

```text
Subject + had + V3
```

Used when one past action happened before another past action.

```text
The train had left before we reached the station.
```

Earlier action:

```text
train had left
```

Later action:

```text
we reached
```

---

## Future Simple

### Structure

```text
Subject + will + V1
```

Example:

```text
I will study tomorrow.
```

---

## Tense Shortcut

| Tense                      | Structure              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Present Simple             | V1 / V1+s              |
| Present Continuous         | is/am/are + V-ing      |
| Present Perfect            | has/have + V3          |
| Present Perfect Continuous | has/have been + V-ing  |
| Past Simple                | V2                     |
| Past Continuous            | was/were + V-ing       |
| Past Perfect               | had + V3               |
| Past Perfect Continuous    | had been + V-ing       |
| Future Simple              | will + V1              |
| Future Continuous          | will be + V-ing        |
| Future Perfect             | will have + V3         |
| Future Perfect Continuous  | will have been + V-ing |

# 4. Articles

There are three articles:

```text
a
an
the
```

---

## A

Use `a` before a singular countable noun beginning with a **consonant sound**.

```text
a book
a car
a student
a university
```

Notice:

```text
university
```

starts with the sound `/y/`, so:

```text
a university
```

---

## An

Use `an` before a singular countable noun beginning with a **vowel sound**.

```text
an apple
an engineer
an umbrella
an hour
```

`hour` starts with a vowel sound because the `h` is silent.

```text
an hour
```

---

## The

Use `the` when referring to something specific or already known.

```text
I saw a dog.
The dog was black.
```

First mention:

```text
a dog
```

Second/specific mention:

```text
the dog
```

---

## The with unique things

```text
the Sun
the Earth
the Moon
```

---

## The with superlatives

```text
the best
the tallest
the most intelligent
```

Example:

```text
He is the best player in the team.
```

---

## Common Article Trap

```text
He is an honest man. ✅
```

Not:

```text
He is a honest man. ❌
```

Because `honest` begins with a vowel sound.

---

# 5. Prepositions

Prepositions are highly important in error-spotting questions.

---

## In vs On vs At

### Time

```text
at 5 PM
on Monday
in January
in 2026
```

Think:

```text
AT → exact time
ON → day/date
IN → month/year/long period
```

---

## Place

```text
at the station
on the table
in the room
```

General idea:

```text
AT → point
ON → surface
IN → enclosed area
```

---

## Since vs For

### Since

Used with a **starting point**.

```text
since Monday
since 2020
since morning
```

### For

Used with a **duration**.

```text
for two hours
for three years
for a week
```

### Example

```text
I have lived here since 2020.
I have lived here for six years.
```

---

## Between vs Among

### Between

Traditionally used for two entities.

```text
Divide the money between Ram and Shyam.
```

It can also be used for more than two when referring to distinct individual relationships or choices.

### Among

Used for a group or collective setting.

```text
She distributed the sweets among the students.
```

---

## By vs With

### By

Often indicates the agent or means/method.

```text
The book was written by him.
I travelled by train.
```

### With

Often indicates the instrument/tool.

```text
He cut the fruit with a knife.
```

---

# 6. Pronouns

A pronoun must correctly agree with the noun it refers to.

---

## Pronoun Agreement

```text
Every student must bring his or her ID.
```

In modern English, singular `they` is also widely used:

```text
Every student must bring their ID.
```

For aptitude questions, follow the grammar convention expected by the question and context.

---

## Subject vs Object Pronouns

### Subject

```text
I
he
she
we
they
```

### Object

```text
me
him
her
us
them
```

Example:

```text
She and I went to the market. ✅
```

Not:

```text
Her and me went to the market. ❌
```

---

## Reflexive Pronouns

```text
myself
yourself
himself
herself
itself
ourselves
themselves
```

Used when the subject and object refer to the same person/thing.

```text
He hurt himself.
She introduced herself.
```

---

## Common Trap

Do not use reflexive pronouns unnecessarily.

```text
Divyansh and I completed the project. ✅
```

Not:

```text
Divyansh and myself completed the project. ❌
```

---

# 7. Conjunctions

Conjunctions connect words, phrases, or clauses.

---

## And

Adds information.

```text
He studied and practiced.
```

---

## But

Shows contrast.

```text
He studied hard, but he failed.
```

---

## Or

Shows alternatives.

```text
You can call me or message me.
```

---

## Because

Shows reason.

```text
He stayed home because he was sick.
```

---

## Although

Shows contrast.

```text
Although he was tired, he continued working.
```

### Common Trap

Do not normally combine `although` and `but` for the same contrast construction.

```text
Although he was tired, he continued working. ✅
```

```text
Although he was tired, but he continued working. ❌
```

---

## Unless

Means essentially:

```text
if not
```

Example:

```text
You will fail unless you study.
```

Equivalent idea:

```text
You will fail if you do not study.
```

---

## Correlative Conjunctions

Used in pairs:

```text
either...or
neither...nor
both...and
not only...but also
whether...or
```

Example:

```text
She is both intelligent and hardworking.
```

---

# 8. Adjectives and Adverbs

## Adjective vs Adverb

Compare:

```text
He is a careful driver.
```

`careful` describes the noun `driver`.

```text
He drives carefully.
```

`carefully` describes the verb `drives`.

---

## Comparative Degree

Used when comparing two.

```text
tall → taller
fast → faster
```

Example:

```text
Ram is taller than Shyam.
```

---

## Superlative Degree

Used when comparing three or more.

```text
tall → tallest
fast → fastest
```

Example:

```text
Ram is the tallest student in the class.
```

---

## Double Comparative Trap

Do not use:

```text
more better ❌
more faster ❌
most fastest ❌
```

Correct:

```text
better ✅
faster ✅
fastest ✅
```

---

## Fewer vs Less

### Fewer

Used with countable nouns.

```text
fewer students
fewer books
fewer mistakes
```

### Less

Used with uncountable nouns.

```text
less water
less money
less time
```

---

## Much vs Many

### Many

Countable nouns:

```text
many students
many books
many questions
```

### Much

Uncountable nouns:

```text
much water
much information
much money
```

---

# 9. Common Grammar Traps

These are especially useful for **error spotting and sentence correction**.

---

## 1. Information

`Information` is generally uncountable.

```text
Correct:
some information
a piece of information
much information
```

Not:

```text
informations ❌
an information ❌
```

---

## 2. Advice

`Advice` is generally uncountable.

```text
some advice
a piece of advice
```

Not:

```text
advices ❌
an advice ❌
```

---

## 3. Furniture

```text
The furniture is expensive. ✅
```

Not:

```text
The furniture are expensive. ❌
```

---

## 4. News

Although it ends in `s`:

```text
The news is shocking. ✅
```

Not:

```text
The news are shocking. ❌
```

---

## 5. Its vs It's

### Its

Possessive form.

```text
The company changed its policy.
```

### It's

Short form of:

```text
it is
```

or

```text
it has
```

Example:

```text
It's raining.
It's been a long day.
```

---

## 6. Their / There / They're

### Their

Possession.

```text
Their project is complete.
```

### There

Place/existence.

```text
The book is there.
There is a problem.
```

### They're

Short form of:

```text
they are
```

```text
They're working.
```

---

## 7. Affect vs Effect

### Affect

Usually a verb meaning to influence.

```text
Lack of sleep affects concentration.
```

### Effect

Usually a noun meaning result.

```text
Lack of sleep has an effect on concentration.
```

---

## 8. Lay vs Lie

### Lay

Usually takes an object.

```text
Lay the book on the table.
```

### Lie

Means to recline/rest.

```text
I want to lie down.
```

---

## 9. Who vs Whom

### Who

Usually functions as a subject.

```text
Who called you?
```

### Whom

Traditionally functions as an object.

```text
Whom did you meet?
```

Quick test:

```text
he → who
him → whom
```

---

## 10. Then vs Than

### Then

Time/sequence.

```text
Study first, then practice.
```

### Than

Comparison.

```text
Java is easier than C++ for me.
```

---

## 11. Your vs You're

### Your

Possession.

```text
Your laptop is here.
```

### You're

You are.

```text
You're doing well.
```

---

## 12. Principal vs Principle

### Principal

Main/important person or amount.

```text
The principal addressed the students.
```

### Principle

A rule or fundamental belief.

```text
Honesty is an important principle.
```

---

# 10. Quick Error-Spotting Checklist

When solving an error-spotting question, don't randomly inspect every word.

Use this order:

```text
Sentence
   ↓
1. Find the Subject
   ↓
2. Check Subject–Verb Agreement
   ↓
3. Check Tense
   ↓
4. Check Articles
   ↓
5. Check Prepositions
   ↓
6. Check Pronouns
   ↓
7. Check Adjective/Adverb
   ↓
8. Check Conjunction
   ↓
9. Check Word Usage
   ↓
10. Check Overall Meaning
```

### Example

> The quality of these products **are** very good.

First identify the subject:

```text
The quality
```

Not:

```text
products
```

`quality` is singular.

Therefore:

```text
is
```

Correct sentence:

```text
The quality of these products is very good.
```

# 11. Top 10 Interview/Aptitude Rules

### Rule 1

**Each / Every → singular noun + singular verb**

```text
Every student is ready.
```

### Rule 2

**Words between subject and verb do not change the subject.**

```text
The quality of the products is good.
```

### Rule 3

**Along with / as well as / together with do not make the subject plural.**

```text
The manager, along with his employees, is present.
```

### Rule 4

**Since → starting point**

```text
since 2020
since Monday
```

**For → duration**

```text
for five years
for two hours
```

### Rule 5

**A/An depends on sound, not spelling.**

```text
a university
an hour
```

### Rule 6

**Present perfect + finished specific past time is generally incorrect.**

```text
I finished it yesterday. ✅
```

### Rule 7

**Much → uncountable, Many → countable**

```text
much money
many students
```

### Rule 8

**Fewer → countable, Less → uncountable**

```text
fewer mistakes
less time
```

### Rule 9

**Although and but should not normally be paired in the same contrast construction.**

```text
Although he was tired, he worked. ✅
```

### Rule 10

**Always identify the actual subject before selecting the verb.**

This single habit eliminates many error-spotting mistakes.

---

# 12. 30-Second Revision

```text
Subject → find the real subject first.

Verb → make it agree with the subject.

Tense → check when the action happened.

A/An → check the SOUND.

The → specific/known/unique things.

Since → starting point.

For → duration.

Much → uncountable.

Many → countable.

Less → uncountable.

Fewer → countable.

Who → subject.

Whom → object.

Its → possession.

It's → it is / it has.

Their → possession.

There → place/existence.

They're → they are.

Affect → usually verb.

Effect → usually noun.

Then → time/sequence.

Than → comparison.
```

---

## 🧠 Memory Trick

For **error spotting**, remember:

> **S-V-T-A-P-P-C-W**

```text
S → Subject
V → Verb
T → Tense
A → Articles
P → Prepositions
P → Pronouns
C → Conjunctions
W → Word usage
```

Follow this order instead of checking the sentence randomly.

```text

★★★★☆ Conjunctions
```
