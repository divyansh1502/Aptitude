# 📘 Grammar Basics: Notes

> **Why this matters:** Grammar feeds directly into Error Spotting, Sentence Correction, Fill in the Blanks and part of Reading Comprehension. TCS iON officially lists *English Grammar* in its Cognitive syllabus, and every other IT drive tests it too.

**Topics covered:** Parts of Speech · Tenses · Subject-Verb Agreement · Articles · Prepositions · Pronouns · Modifiers

---

## 1. Parts of Speech

Every word in a sentence does one of eight jobs.

| Part | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Noun** | Names a person, place, thing or idea | Rahul, Pune, laptop, honesty |
| **Pronoun** | Replaces a noun | he, she, it, they, who |
| **Verb** | Shows action or state | run, is, write, seem |
| **Adjective** | Describes a noun | tall boy, *three* books |
| **Adverb** | Describes a verb, adjective or another adverb | runs *quickly*, *very* tall |
| **Preposition** | Shows relation of a noun to another word | in, on, at, by, with |
| **Conjunction** | Joins words or clauses | and, but, because, although |
| **Interjection** | Expresses sudden emotion | Oh! Wow! Alas! |

**Tip:** The same word can change its part of speech depending on the sentence.

- "I need a *drink*." → noun
- "I *drink* tea." → verb
- "She works *hard*." → adverb
- "It is a *hard* problem." → adjective

## 2. Tenses

A tense tells you **when** something happens and **what state** it is in (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous).

| Tense | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | V1 (+s/es for he/she/it) | She writes daily. |
| Present Continuous | is/am/are + V-ing | She is writing now. |
| Present Perfect | has/have + V3 | She has written a report. |
| Present Perfect Continuous | has/have been + V-ing | She has been writing for an hour. |
| Simple Past | V2 | She wrote a report. |
| Past Continuous | was/were + V-ing | She was writing when I called. |
| Past Perfect | had + V3 | She had written it before I came. |
| Past Perfect Continuous | had been + V-ing | She had been writing for an hour. |
| Simple Future | will + V1 | She will write tomorrow. |
| Future Continuous | will be + V-ing | She will be writing at 5 PM. |
| Future Perfect | will have + V3 | She will have written it by Friday. |
| Future Perfect Continuous | will have been + V-ing | She will have been writing for two hours. |

### When to use which

- **Simple present:** habits, facts, schedules. *The sun rises in the east.*
- **Present perfect:** past action with a link to now, and no fixed past time. *I have lost my keys.*
- **Simple past:** finished action at a definite past time. *I lost my keys yesterday.*
- **Past perfect:** the **earlier** of two past actions. *The train had left before I reached.*
- **Continuous tenses:** an action in progress at a particular time.

### Signal words (very useful in exams)

| Signal | Usually points to |
|---|---|
| since, for, so far, yet, already, just, ever, never | Present perfect |
| yesterday, ago, last week, in 2019 | Simple past |
| by the time, before, after (two past events) | Past perfect for the earlier event |
| by next week, by then (future deadline) | Future perfect |
| now, at the moment, currently | Present continuous |

### Time clauses with *if / when / before / after / as soon as*

Do **not** use *will* in the time or condition clause. Use the present tense.

- ✅ *I will call you when I **reach** home.*
- ❌ *I will call you when I will reach home.*

## 3. Subject-Verb Agreement

The verb must match its subject in **number** (singular or plural).

- Singular subject → singular verb: *The boy **plays**.*
- Plural subject → plural verb: *The boys **play**.*

The hard part is finding the **real subject**. Ignore words that sit between the subject and the verb.

- *The **list** of items **is** on the table.* (subject = list, not items)
- *The **boxes** in the store **are** damaged.* (subject = boxes)

### Key situations

1. **Each, every, either, neither, one of, everyone, somebody** → singular.
   *Each of the players **was** given a medal.*
2. **Either…or / Neither…nor / Not only…but also** → verb agrees with the **nearer** subject.
   *Neither the manager nor the employees **were** aware.*
3. **With / along with / as well as / together with / in addition to** → verb agrees with the **first** subject.
   *The teacher, along with her students, **is** coming.*
4. **A number of** → plural. **The number of** → singular.
5. **Many a** + singular noun → singular verb. *Many a student **has** failed.*
6. **Uncountable nouns** are singular: furniture, information, advice, news, luggage, equipment, knowledge, scenery.
7. **Collective nouns** (team, committee, family): singular when acting as one unit, plural when members act separately.
8. **Plural-only nouns** take plural verbs: scissors, trousers, spectacles, people, police.
9. **Subjects joined by *and*** are plural. *Ram and Shyam **are** friends.*
   Exception: when they form one idea. *Bread and butter **is** my breakfast.*
10. **Distance, money and time as one unit** → singular. *Ten kilometres **is** a long walk.*

## 4. Articles (a, an, the)

| Article | Use |
|---|---|
| **a** | Before a **consonant sound**, for a singular, non-specific noun |
| **an** | Before a **vowel sound**, for a singular, non-specific noun |
| **the** | For something specific, unique or already mentioned |
| **no article** | General plural or uncountable nouns, languages, meals, most sports |

**The sound matters, not the spelling.**

- *an hour, an honest man, an MBA, an umbrella*
- *a university, a European, a one-way ticket, a unique idea*

**Use *the* with:** superlatives (*the best*), ordinals (*the first*), rivers, oceans, mountain ranges (*the Ganga, the Himalayas*), unique things (*the sun, the moon*).

**Skip *the* with:** languages (*I speak Hindi*), meals (*have lunch*), most countries and cities, abstract nouns used generally (*Honesty is the best policy*).

## 5. Prepositions

Prepositions are mostly about **fixed usage**, so learn them in pairs.

### Time and place

| | at | on | in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time** | exact point: *at 5 PM, at noon, at night* | days and dates: *on Monday, on 5 May* | longer periods: *in May, in 2024, in summer, in the morning* |
| **Place** | specific point: *at the door, at the station* | surface: *on the table, on the wall* | enclosed space: *in the room, in Pune* |

### Since vs for

- **since** + starting point: *since 2019, since Monday*
- **for** + duration: *for two hours, for five years*

### Fixed combinations (learn these)

| Adjective / verb | Preposition |
|---|---|
| good / bad / weak at | at |
| interested / involved in | in |
| afraid / fond / proud / capable / aware of | of |
| married / similar / superior / inferior / addicted to | to |
| different from | from |
| depend / insist / rely on | on |
| consist of | of |
| divided into | into |
| responsible for | for |
| prefer X **to** Y | to |

**Verbs that take no preposition:** *discuss* (not discuss about), *enter* (not enter into a room), *reach* (not reach to), *return* (not return back), *marry* (not marry with).

## 6. Pronouns

A pronoun replaces a noun and must agree with it in number, person and case.

| | Subject | Object | Possessive | Reflexive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st person | I / we | me / us | my, mine / our, ours | myself / ourselves |
| 2nd person | you | you | your, yours | yourself / yourselves |
| 3rd person | he, she, it, they | him, her, it, them | his, her(s), its, their(s) | himself, herself, itself, themselves |

### Rules that get tested

1. **Case after prepositions:** use the object form. *Between you and **me**.* (not "you and I")
2. **Agreement:** *Each of the students must submit **his or her** assignment.* Each, everyone and anyone are singular.
3. **Clear reference:** avoid vague pronouns. ❌ *Raj told Amit that **he** was late.* (who?)
4. **Who vs whom:** *who* does the action (subject), *whom* receives it (object). *Whom did you call?*
5. **Reflexive pronouns** are used only when the subject and object are the same. ❌ *Please send it to Ravi and myself.* ✅ *…to Ravi and me.*
6. **Its vs it's:** *its* = belonging to it; *it's* = it is / it has.

## 7. Modifiers

A modifier describes something. It must sit **next to the word it describes**.

### Misplaced modifier
- ❌ *I saw a dog on my way to school **with one eye**.*
- ✅ *On my way to school, I saw a dog **with one eye**.*

### Dangling modifier (the thing being described is missing)
- ❌ *Walking down the road, **a dog bit me**.* (a dog was not walking)
- ✅ *While I was walking down the road, a dog bit me.*
- ✅ *Walking down the road, **I** was bitten by a dog.*

**Test:** Ask "Who is doing the action in the opening phrase?" The answer must be the subject right after the comma.

### Only
*Only* goes right before the word it limits, and moving it changes the meaning.
- *Only I love you.* (nobody else does)
- *I only love you.* (I do nothing else to you)
- *I love only you.* (nobody but you)

## 8. Worked Examples (easy → exam level)

**Example 1 (Easy): Parts of speech**
Identify the part of speech of *beautifully* in "She sings beautifully."
→ It describes the verb *sings*, so it is an **adverb**.

**Example 2 (Easy): Article**
Fill in: "He is ___ honest man."
→ *Honest* starts with a vowel **sound** (silent h), so **an**.

**Example 3 (Medium): Subject-verb agreement**
Choose: "The quality of the apples ___ poor."
→ The real subject is *quality*, not *apples*. Singular → **is**.

**Example 4 (Medium): Tense**
Choose: "By the time we reached, the film ___."
→ Two past events, and the film started earlier. Earlier event → past perfect → **had started**.

**Example 5 (Exam level): Error spotting**
"Neither of the answers (A) were correct, (B) so I (C) lost marks (D)."
→ *Neither of* is singular. Error in (A). Correct: **was correct**.

**Example 6 (Exam level): Preposition + pronoun**
Correct the sentence: "He is senior than me and prefers tea than coffee."
→ *Senior* takes **to**, and *prefer* takes **to**.
✅ *He is senior **to** me and prefers tea **to** coffee.*

## 9. Common Question Patterns

| Pattern | What they ask |
|---|---|
| **Error spotting** | Find which part of the sentence is wrong |
| **Fill in the blanks** | Choose the correct verb form, article or preposition |
| **Sentence correction** | Pick the best rewrite of an underlined part |
| **Identify the part of speech** | Name the underlined word's role |
| **Tense conversion** | Rewrite in another tense |

## 10. Traps to Watch For

- A **long phrase between subject and verb** hides the real subject.
- **Collective nouns** and **uncountable nouns** (furniture, information) look plural but are singular.
- **Spelling vs sound** for *a/an* (*an hour*, *a university*).
- **Since vs for**: starting point vs duration.
- **"Will" inside an *if/when* clause** is almost always wrong.
- Two options that look nearly identical: check the **verb form**, then the **preposition**, then the **article**, in that order.

---

## 11. Quick Exam Method

1. Find the **subject** and the **verb**. Do they agree?
2. Check the **tense** against any signal words.
3. Check **pronoun** number and case.
4. Check **articles and prepositions**.
5. Read the final sentence once. If it sounds natural and every rule above passes, mark it.
