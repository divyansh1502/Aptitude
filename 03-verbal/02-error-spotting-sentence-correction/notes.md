# 1. Error Spotting

In error-spotting questions, a sentence is divided into parts. One part contains a grammatical or usage error.

### Example

> The list of students / **are** displayed / on the notice board.

The error is **are**.

Correct:

> The list of students **is** displayed on the notice board.

### Common Error Areas

```text
Subject–Verb Agreement
Tenses
Articles
Prepositions
Pronouns
Adjectives/Adverbs
Comparison
Parallelism
Modifiers
Conjunctions
Word Usage
Redundancy
Conditionals
```

# 2. Sentence Correction

Sentence correction questions provide an incorrect sentence and ask you to select the grammatically correct version.

### Example

> She is more better than her sister. ❌

Correct:

> She is better than her sister. ✅

### Important

Do not choose an option simply because it sounds natural.

Check:

1. Grammar
2. Tense
3. Meaning
4. Word usage
5. Conciseness

# 3. Subject–Verb Agreement

The verb must agree with the **real subject**, not necessarily the nearest noun.

## Basic Rule

```text
Singular subject → singular verb
Plural subject   → plural verb
```

Examples:

> The student **is** ready.

> The students **are** ready.

---

## Prepositional Phrase Trap

> The list of students **is** ready.

The subject is **list**, not students.

Similarly:

> The quality of these products **is** excellent.

### Shortcut

Ignore phrases beginning with:

```text
of
with
along with
as well as
together with
```

and identify the main subject.

---

## Each / Every

These normally take singular verbs.

> Each student **has** an ID.

> Every candidate **is** eligible.

---

## Everyone / Someone / Nobody

These are treated as singular.

> Everyone **is** ready.

> Nobody **knows** the answer.

---

## A Number vs The Number

### A number of

Means several.

> A number of students **are** absent.

### The number of

Refers to the total number.

> The number of students **is** increasing.

---

## Either...Or / Neither...Nor

The verb generally agrees with the subject closer to it.

> Neither the manager nor the employees **are** ready.

> Neither the employees nor the manager **is** ready.

---

## Along With / As Well As

These do not make the main subject plural.

> The manager, along with his employees, **is** attending.

---

# 4. Tense Errors

Always check whether the tense matches the time and sequence of events.

## Simple Present

Used for:

* habits
* facts
* general truths

> He works every day.

---

## Simple Past

Used for completed past actions.

> She visited Delhi last year.

---

## Present Perfect

```text
has/have + V3
```

Used for past actions connected to the present or unspecified past experiences.

> I have completed the task.

---

## Past Perfect

```text
had + V3
```

Used when one past action happened before another past action.

> The train had left before we reached the station.

Timeline:

```text
Train left
    ↓
We reached
```

---

## Since vs For

### Since

Specific starting point.

> since 2022

> since Monday

### For

Duration.

> for five years

> for two months

---

## Finished Past Time

Do not normally use present perfect with a finished time expression such as:

> yesterday

> last year

> two days ago

Correct:

> I completed the work yesterday.

Not:

> I have completed the work yesterday.

---

# 5. Article Errors

Articles:

```text
a
an
the
```

## A

Used before a consonant sound.

> a university

> a European

> a useful idea

---

## An

Used before a vowel sound.

> an apple

> an honest man

> an hour

> an MBA

### Important

The rule depends on **sound**, not spelling.

---

## The

Commonly used for:

* specific nouns
* previously mentioned nouns
* unique things

Examples:

> the Sun

> the Earth

> the book you gave me

---

## No Article

Often used when speaking generally about:

* plural nouns
* uncountable nouns
* abstract concepts

Examples:

> Students need practice.

> Honesty is important.

> Information is valuable.

---

# 6. Preposition Errors

Many prepositions are tested through fixed combinations.

## Common Combinations

```text
good at
interested in
capable of
responsible for
depend on
belong to
consist of
similar to
different from
superior to
inferior to
senior to
```

Examples:

> She is good **at** mathematics.

> He is interested **in** Java.

> The result depends **on** the input.

---

## Time Prepositions

```text
at → exact time
on → day/date
in → month/year/long period
```

Examples:

> at 6 PM

> on Monday

> on 15 August

> in 2026

---

## Common Errors

Incorrect:

> Discuss about the issue. ❌

Correct:

> Discuss the issue. ✅

Incorrect:

> Enter into the room. ❌

Correct:

> Enter the room. ✅

Incorrect:

> Explain me the problem. ❌

Correct:

> Explain the problem to me. ✅

---

# 7. Pronoun Errors

Pronouns must agree with and clearly refer to their nouns.

## Subject Pronouns

```text
I
we
you
he
she
they
```

> Rahul and **I** went to the library.

---

## Object Pronouns

```text
me
us
you
him
her
them
```

> The teacher called Rahul and **me**.

---

## Who vs Whom

### Who

Subject.

> The student **who** won the competition is my friend.

### Whom

Object in formal usage.

> Whom did you meet?

---

## Ambiguous Pronoun

> Rahul told Aman that he was selected.

Who was selected?

The pronoun **he** is unclear.

In formal writing, make the reference clear.

---

# 8. Adjective and Adverb Errors

## Adjective

Describes a noun.

> He is a **careful** driver.

## Adverb

Usually describes a verb.

> He drives **carefully**.

### Common Error

> She completed the task quick. ❌

> She completed the task quickly. ✅

---

# 9. Comparison Errors

## Comparative

Used mainly for comparing two things.

```text
taller than
better than
more useful than
```

Example:

> This method is better than that one.

---

## Superlative

Used when comparing three or more.

```text
the tallest
the best
the most useful
```

---

## Double Comparison

Incorrect:

> more better ❌

> more easier ❌

> most fastest ❌

Correct:

> better

> easier

> fastest

---

## One of the

Pattern:

```text
one of the + superlative + plural noun
```

Correct:

> He is one of the best **students**.

Incorrect:

> He is one of the best **student**. ❌

---

# 10. Parallelism

Items connected in a sentence should have similar grammatical structures.

### Incorrect

> She likes reading, writing, and to travel. ❌

### Correct

> She likes reading, writing, and travelling. ✅

---

## Correlative Conjunctions

```text
both...and
either...or
neither...nor
not only...but also
```

Keep the structures parallel.

Example:

> He is not only intelligent but also hardworking.

---

# 11. Modifier Errors

A modifier should be placed close to the word it describes.

### Incorrect

> Walking to college, the rain started.

The rain wasn't walking.

### Correct

> Walking to college, I got caught in the rain.

### Quick Test

Ask:

> **Who is performing the action in the opening phrase?**

---

# 12. Conjunction Errors

## Although + But

Don't normally use both together.

Incorrect:

> Although he was tired, but he continued working. ❌

Correct:

> Although he was tired, he continued working. ✅

Or:

> He was tired, but he continued working. ✅

---

## Unless

Unless means **if not**.

> Unless you study, you will not pass.

Avoid unnecessary double negatives.

---

# 13. Redundancy

Redundancy means unnecessarily repeating the same meaning.

### Common Examples

```text
return back ❌
repeat again ❌
discuss about ❌
revert back ❌
past history ❌
free gift ❌
advance planning ❌
```

Better:

```text
return
repeat
discuss
revert
history
gift
planning
```

---

# 14. Commonly Confused Words

## Affect vs Effect

```text
affect → usually verb
effect → usually noun
```

> The change will **affect** the result.

> The change had an **effect** on performance.

---

## Its vs It's

```text
its → possession
it's → it is / it has
```

---

## Your vs You're

```text
your → possession
you're → you are
```

---

## Their / There / They're

```text
their → possession
there → place/existence
they're → they are
```

---

## Then vs Than

```text
then → time/order
than → comparison
```

---

## Advice vs Advise

```text
advice → noun
advise → verb
```

> He gave me good **advice**.

> I **advise** you to practice.

---

# 15. Conditional Errors

## First Conditional

Possible future condition:

```text
If + present simple → will + V1
```

> If I study, I will pass.

---

## Second Conditional

Hypothetical/unreal situation:

```text
If + past simple → would + V1
```

> If I had more time, I would practice more.

Special form:

> If I **were** you...

---

## Third Conditional

Past unreal situation:

```text
If + had + V3 → would have + V3
```

> If I had studied, I would have passed.

---

# 16. Countable and Uncountable Nouns

### Countable

Can be counted.

> one student

> two students

Use:

```text
many
few
a few
several
```

### Uncountable

Generally cannot be counted individually.

```text
information
advice
equipment
furniture
knowledge
```

Use:

```text
much
little
a little
some
```

Incorrect:

> informations ❌

> advices ❌

> equipments ❌

# 17. Error Spotting Strategy

Don't inspect every word randomly.

Use this order:

```text
Subject
   ↓
Verb
   ↓
Tense
   ↓
Article
   ↓
Preposition
   ↓
Pronoun
   ↓
Modifier
   ↓
Parallelism
   ↓
Comparison
   ↓
Word Usage
```

### Powerful Trick

Reduce complicated sentences.

Original:

> The list of students who applied for the internship is available.

Mentally reduce:

> The list is available.

Now the subject becomes obvious.

# 18. Sentence Correction Strategy

### Step 1

Read the complete sentence.

### Step 2

Identify the suspicious section.

### Step 3

Determine the grammar rule.

### Step 4

Eliminate options that:

* change the meaning,
* introduce another error,
* contain redundancy,
* break parallelism,
* use the wrong tense.

### Step 5

Choose the grammatically correct and concise option.
