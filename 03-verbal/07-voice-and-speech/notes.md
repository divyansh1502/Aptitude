# 🗣️ Voice and Speech

> **Voice** tells whether the subject performs or receives an action. **Speech** tells how someone's words are reported.

---

# 📌 Topic Overview

This topic has two major parts:

```text
VOICE
├── Active Voice
└── Passive Voice

SPEECH
├── Direct Speech
└── Indirect / Reported Speech
```

---

# 🟢 PART 1 — ACTIVE AND PASSIVE VOICE

## What is Voice?

Voice shows the relationship between the **subject** and the **action**.

### Active Voice

The subject performs the action.

```text
Subject + Verb + Object
```

Example:

> The developer wrote the program.

```text
Subject = The developer
Verb    = wrote
Object  = the program
```

---

### Passive Voice

The subject receives the action.

```text
Object + Helping Verb + V3 + by + Subject
```

Example:

> The program was written by the developer.

---

# 🔄 Active → Passive

Basic transformation:

```text
ACTIVE
Subject → Verb → Object

        ↓

PASSIVE
Object → Helping Verb + V3 → by + Subject
```

Example:

```text
Active:
The teacher checked the assignments.

Passive:
The assignments were checked by the teacher.
```

---

# 🧠 The Most Important Rule

When converting Active → Passive:

```text
1. Object becomes subject.
2. Main verb becomes V3.
3. Helping verb changes according to tense.
4. Original subject moves after "by".
5. Pronouns may change.
```

---

# 📚 Verb Forms

```text
V1 = base form
V2 = past form
V3 = past participle
```

Example:

```text
write → wrote → written
eat   → ate   → eaten
do    → did   → done
make  → made  → made
```

Passive voice uses:

```text
V3
```

---

# ⏱️ Tense-Wise Active → Passive

## 1. Simple Present

### Active

```text
S + V1/V1(s) + O
```

### Passive

```text
O + am/is/are + V3 + by + S
```

Example:

> The manager approves the request.

→ The request is approved by the manager.

---

# 2. Present Continuous

### Active

```text
S + am/is/are + V-ing + O
```

### Passive

```text
O + am/is/are + being + V3
```

Example:

> The team is developing the application.

→ The application is being developed by the team.

---

# 3. Present Perfect

### Active

```text
S + has/have + V3 + O
```

### Passive

```text
O + has/have + been + V3
```

Example:

> The company has completed the project.

→ The project has been completed by the company.

---

# 4. Simple Past

### Active

```text
S + V2 + O
```

### Passive

```text
O + was/were + V3
```

Example:

> The engineer fixed the server.

→ The server was fixed by the engineer.

---

# 5. Past Continuous

### Active

```text
S + was/were + V-ing + O
```

### Passive

```text
O + was/were + being + V3
```

Example:

> The workers were repairing the road.

→ The road was being repaired by the workers.

---

# 6. Past Perfect

### Active

```text
S + had + V3 + O
```

### Passive

```text
O + had + been + V3
```

Example:

> The team had completed the task.

→ The task had been completed by the team.

---

# 7. Simple Future

### Active

```text
S + will + V1 + O
```

### Passive

```text
O + will + be + V3
```

Example:

> The company will release the results.

→ The results will be released by the company.

---

# 8. Future Perfect

### Active

```text
S + will have + V3 + O
```

### Passive

```text
O + will have been + V3
```

Example:

> They will have completed the project.

→ The project will have been completed by them.

---

# ⚠️ Tenses Usually Not Converted Normally

Some tense forms are grammatically possible but are generally avoided in standard aptitude conversion questions because the passive construction becomes awkward.

Examples:

```text
Present Perfect Continuous
Past Perfect Continuous
Future Continuous
Future Perfect Continuous
```

Focus first on the common examinable structures.

# ❓ Passive Questions

## Active

> Did the manager approve the request?

Passive:

> Was the request approved by the manager?

---

## Active

> Will the company release the results?

Passive:

> Will the results be released by the company?

---

## WH Questions

### Active

> Who wrote this program?

Passive:

> By whom was this program written?

---

### Active

> Who is preparing the report?

Passive:

> By whom is the report being prepared?

---

# 🚫 "BY" Is Not Always Required

If the doer is:

* unknown
* obvious
* unimportant
* generic

we often omit it.

Example:

> Someone stole my phone.

→ My phone was stolen.

Not necessarily:

> My phone was stolen by someone.

---

# 🧠 Modal Verbs

Common modals:

```text
can
could
may
might
must
should
would
```

### Active

```text
S + modal + V1 + O
```

### Passive

```text
O + modal + be + V3
```

Example:

> You should complete the assignment.

→ The assignment should be completed by you.

---

# 🔥 Imperative Sentences

Commands and requests require special structures.

### Active

> Open the door.

Passive:

> Let the door be opened.

---

### Active

> Complete the assignment.

Passive:

> Let the assignment be completed.

# 🟣 PART 2 — DIRECT AND INDIRECT SPEECH

## What is Direct Speech?

Direct speech reports the exact words spoken.

Example:

> Rahul said, "I am tired."

The exact words are inside quotation marks.

---

# What is Indirect Speech?

Indirect speech reports the meaning without quoting the exact words.

Example:

> Rahul said that he was tired.

---

# 🔄 Direct → Indirect

When converting Direct → Indirect, check:

```text
1. Reporting verb
2. Tense
3. Pronoun
4. Time/place expressions
5. Sentence type
6. Quotation marks
```

---

# 🧠 Three Major Changes

```text
TENSE
PRONOUN
TIME/PLACE
```

---

# 1️⃣ Tense Backshift

When the reporting verb is in the past, tense commonly moves one step back.

| Direct                     | Indirect                |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Present Simple             | Past Simple             |
| Present Continuous         | Past Continuous         |
| Present Perfect            | Past Perfect            |
| Present Perfect Continuous | Past Perfect Continuous |
| Simple Past                | Past Perfect            |
| Past Continuous            | Past Perfect Continuous |
| Past Perfect               | Past Perfect            |
| Past Perfect Continuous    | Past Perfect Continuous |
| will                       | would                   |
| can                        | could                   |
| may                        | might                   |

---

# Examples

### Present Simple

> He said, "I work here."

→ He said that he worked there.

---

### Present Continuous

> She said, "I am studying."

→ She said that she was studying.

---

### Present Perfect

> He said, "I have completed the task."

→ He said that he had completed the task.

---

### Simple Past

> She said, "I visited Delhi."

→ She said that she had visited Delhi.

---

### Future

> He said, "I will call you."

→ He said that he would call me.

---

# ⚠️ Universal Truth

If the statement expresses a universal truth, the tense generally remains unchanged.

Example:

> The teacher said, "The Earth revolves around the Sun."

→ The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun.

---

# 2️⃣ Pronoun Changes

Pronouns change according to the speaker and listener.

Example:

> Rahul said, "I am busy."

→ Rahul said that **he** was busy.

---

Example:

> Priya said to me, "You are late."

→ Priya told me that **I** was late.

---

# 🧠 Pronoun Shortcut

Ask:

```text
Who is speaking?
Who is being spoken to?
Who/what is being discussed?
```

Then adjust:

```text
I → speaker
you → listener
he/she/they → usually remains according to context
```

---

# 3️⃣ Time and Place Changes

| Direct    | Indirect           |
| --------- | ------------------ |
| now       | then               |
| today     | that day           |
| tonight   | that night         |
| yesterday | the previous day   |
| tomorrow  | the next day       |
| last week | the previous week  |
| next week | the following week |
| ago       | before             |
| here      | there              |
| this      | that               |
| these     | those              |

---

# 🟡 Reporting Verbs

## Said

> He said, "I am tired."

→ He said that he was tired.

---

## Said to

Usually becomes:

```text
said to + person
        ↓
told + person
```

Example:

> He said to me, "I am busy."

→ He told me that he was busy.

---

# ❓ Indirect Questions

## Yes/No Questions

Use:

```text
if / whether
```

Example:

> He said, "Are you ready?"

→ He asked me if I was ready.

---

## WH Questions

Keep the question word.

Example:

> She said, "Where do you live?"

→ She asked me where I lived.

---

# 🚨 Important

Indirect questions use **statement word order**.

Wrong:

> She asked where did I live.

Correct:

> She asked where I lived.

---

# 🛠️ Commands and Orders

### Direct

> The teacher said to the students, "Open your books."

Indirect:

> The teacher told the students to open their books.

Structure:

```text
told + object + to + V1
```

---

# 🚫 Negative Commands

Direct:

> He said to me, "Do not touch it."

Indirect:

> He told me not to touch it.

Structure:

```text
told + object + not to + V1
```

---

# 🙏 Requests

Direct:

> She said to me, "Please help me."

Indirect:

> She requested me to help her.

Common reporting verbs:

```text
requested
asked
begged
urged
```

---

# 💡 Advice

Direct:

> The doctor said to me, "Take rest."

Indirect:

> The doctor advised me to take rest.

---

# 🎉 Exclamatory Sentences

Direct:

> He said, "What a beautiful place!"

Indirect:

> He exclaimed that it was a very beautiful place.

---

# 🧠 Speech Conversion Algorithm

```text
DIRECT
   ↓
Identify reporting verb
   ↓
Identify sentence type
   ↓
Change pronouns
   ↓
Change tense if required
   ↓
Change time/place words
   ↓
Remove quotation marks
   ↓
Build indirect sentence
```

---

# ⚠️ Common Traps

## Trap 1 — Forgetting "that"

For statements:

> He said that he was tired.

---

## Trap 2 — Using question order

Wrong:

> He asked where was I going.

Correct:

> He asked where I was going.

---

## Trap 3 — Wrong pronoun

> Rahul said, "I am ready."

Wrong:

> Rahul said that I was ready.

Correct:

> Rahul said that he was ready.

---

## Trap 4 — Wrong time expression

```text
today → that day
tomorrow → the next day
yesterday → the previous day
```

---

## Trap 5 — Using "told" without an object

Wrong:

> He told that he was tired.

Correct:

> He said that he was tired.

OR

> He told me that he was tired.

# 🏆 Top 10 Questions

### 1. What is active voice?

A sentence in which the subject performs the action.

### 2. What is passive voice?

A sentence in which the subject receives the action.

### 3. Which verb form is used in passive voice?

V3 / past participle.

### 4. What happens to the object in passive voice?

It generally becomes the subject.

### 5. What is direct speech?

The exact words of the speaker.

### 6. What is indirect speech?

The reported meaning of the speaker's words.

### 7. What are the three major changes in reported speech?

Tense, pronoun, and time/place expressions.

### 8. How are yes/no questions reported?

Using **if** or **whether**.

### 9. How are commands reported?

Usually with:

```text
told + object + to + V1
```

### 10. What is the biggest trap in indirect questions?

Using question word order instead of statement word order.
