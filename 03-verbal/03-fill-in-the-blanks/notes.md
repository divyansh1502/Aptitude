## 1. What Are Fill-in-the-Blanks?

A **fill-in-the-blank** question gives a sentence with one or more missing words.

You must select the option that completes the sentence correctly.

### Example

> The manager was ______ with the employee's performance.

* A. satisfy
* B. satisfied
* C. satisfying
* D. satisfaction

✅ **Answer: B. satisfied**

Why?

The structure is:

```text
was + adjective
```

So **satisfied** fits grammatically.

---

# 2. Types of Questions

### 1. Grammar-Based

Tests:

* tense
* articles
* prepositions
* subject-verb agreement
* pronouns
* verb forms

Example:

> She has been working here _____ 2022.

✅ **since**

---

### 2. Vocabulary-Based

Tests the meaning of words.

> The manager gave a ______ explanation that everyone understood.

* vague
* clear
* confusing
* ambiguous

✅ **clear**

---

### 3. Context-Based

The answer depends on the overall meaning.

> The road was extremely slippery; ______, the driver reduced his speed.

✅ **therefore**

---

### 4. Double-Blank

Two words must fit together.

> The plan looked ______ initially, but its implementation proved ______.

The answer must make sense for **both blanks**.

---

### 5. Collocation-Based

Tests natural word combinations.

Examples:

```text
make a decision
take responsibility
heavy rain
strong argument
deep concern
commit a mistake ❌
make a mistake ✅
```

---

### 6. Cloze-Type Questions

A paragraph contains multiple blanks.

Here you must understand:

```text
sentence → paragraph → overall meaning
```

Don't solve every blank independently.

---

# 3. The Core Solving Method

Use this **5-step method**.

### Step 1 — Read the complete sentence

Don't immediately look at the options.

### Step 2 — Predict the answer

Try to think of your own word.

### Step 3 — Find the clue

Look for:

* but
* although
* because
* therefore
* however
* despite
* since
* while
* unlike
* instead

### Step 4 — Check grammar

Ask:

```text
Is the tense correct?
Is the word form correct?
Is singular/plural correct?
Does the preposition fit?
```

### Step 5 — Check meaning

Even if an option is grammatically correct, it may be logically wrong.

# 4. Grammar-Based Blanks

## 4.1 Subject-Verb Agreement

> Each student _____ required to submit the form.

* are
* were
* is
* have

✅ **is**

Because:

```text
Each + singular noun → singular verb
```

---

## 4.2 Tense

> She _____ to Delhi yesterday.

* goes
* has gone
* went
* going

✅ **went**

Because **yesterday** indicates completed past time.

---

## 4.3 Present Perfect

> I _____ this movie before.

* saw
* have seen
* see
* seeing

✅ **have seen**

No specific completed past time is given.

---

## 4.4 Prepositions

> He is interested _____ programming.

✅ **in**

Common combinations:

```text
interested in
good at
capable of
responsible for
dependent on
similar to
different from
afraid of
famous for
```

---

## 4.5 Articles

> He is _____ honest man.

✅ **an**

Because *honest* begins with a vowel sound.

Remember:

> **Article depends on sound, not spelling.**

---

# 5. Context-Based Blanks

The sentence itself often gives you the answer indirectly.

### Example

> The instructions were so ______ that even beginners understood them easily.

Options:

* confusing
* vague
* clear
* complicated

The clue is:

> **even beginners understood them easily**

Therefore:

✅ **clear**

---

# 6. Clue Words

Clue words are extremely important.

## 🔄 Contrast

These indicate an opposite idea:

```text
but
although
however
yet
despite
whereas
while
nevertheless
```

### Example

> Although the task was difficult, she remained ______.

The blank should have a **positive/strength-related meaning**.

Possible answer:

✅ **confident**

---

## ➡️ Cause

```text
because
since
as
due to
owing to
```

### Example

> He missed the train because he was ______.

✅ **late**

---

## 🎯 Result

```text
therefore
thus
hence
so
consequently
```

### Example

> He worked hard; therefore, he ______ the examination.

✅ **passed**

---

## ➕ Similarity / Addition

```text
and
also
moreover
furthermore
likewise
```

The ideas generally move in the same direction.

---

## ⚖️ Comparison

```text
than
unlike
similar to
whereas
while
```

Look for relationships between the two ideas.

# 7. Tone of the Sentence

Before selecting a vocabulary word, identify the tone.

### Positive

```text
successful
excellent
effective
confident
beneficial
reliable
efficient
```

### Negative

```text
failure
poor
harmful
ineffective
confusing
uncertain
dangerous
```

### Neutral

```text
ordinary
common
normal
standard
typical
```

### Example

> Despite facing several difficulties, the team remained ______ and completed the project.

The phrase **despite difficulties** contrasts with the blank.

So the blank should be positive.

✅ **determined**

# 8. Vocabulary-Based Blanks

Don't select a word only because you know its meaning.

Check its **usage**.

### Example

> The company decided to _____ the new policy.

Possible options:

* implement
* create
* perform
* commit

✅ **implement**

Because:

> implement a policy

is the natural expression.

---

## 🔥 Learn Words in Context

Instead of:

```text
implement = apply
```

Learn:

```text
implement a policy
implement a strategy
implement a plan
implement changes
```

This makes fill-in-the-blank questions much easier.

# 9. Collocations

A **collocation** is a combination of words that naturally occur together.

### Important Placement Collocations

| Correct             | Incorrect           |
| ------------------- | ------------------- |
| make a decision     | do a decision       |
| make a mistake      | do a mistake        |
| take responsibility | make responsibility |
| take action         | do action           |
| pay attention       | give attention      |
| conduct research    | do research         |
| gain experience     | get experience      |
| achieve a goal      | make a goal         |
| solve a problem     | resolve a problem   |
| raise a question    | rise a question     |
| heavy rain          | strong rain         |
| strong argument     | powerful argument*  |
| deep concern        | high concern        |
| highly effective    | highly efficient*   |

> Some alternatives can be grammatically possible but are less natural in standard usage.

# 10. Double Blanks

Double blanks are harder because **both words must fit**.

### Example

> The manager was ______ by the employee's ______ performance.

Possible pair:

```text
impressed / excellent
```

Both work.

---

## Strategy

Never solve only the first blank.

Use:

```text
Blank 1
   ↓
Blank 2
   ↓
Relationship between them
   ↓
Complete sentence
```

### Example

> The proposal was initially ______, but later proved to be ______.

The word after **but** should contrast with the first.

Possible pair:

```text
promising / unsuccessful
```

---

# 11. Phrasal Verb Blanks

Some questions test phrasal verbs.

### Common Examples

```text
look after   → take care of
look into    → investigate
give up      → quit
carry out    → perform
bring up     → mention/raise
find out     → discover
put off      → postpone
call off     → cancel
break down   → stop functioning
set up       → establish
take over    → assume control
```

### Example

> The committee will ______ the matter before making a decision.

✅ **look into**

# 12. Common Traps

## Trap 1 — Grammatically Correct but Wrong Meaning

> The company suffered a ______ increase in profits.

If the options include:

```text
significant
insignificant
```

Both may be grammatically possible.

Context decides the answer.

---

## Trap 2 — Similar Words

```text
affect / effect
accept / except
advice / advise
principal / principle
compliment / complement
stationary / stationery
```

Don't choose based on appearance.

---

## Trap 3 — Wrong Word Form

> She is very ______ at solving problems.

* skill
* skilled
* skillfully
* skills

✅ **skilled**

Structure:

```text
is + adjective
```

---

## Trap 4 — Ignoring the Second Half

> The project seemed easy at first, but ______.

The word after **but** should consider the contrast.

Never solve the blank by reading only the first half.

---

## Trap 5 — Double-Blank Mismatch

An option may fit Blank 1 perfectly but fail Blank 2.

Always test the **complete pair**.

---

# 13. Elimination Technique

When you don't know the answer, eliminate options systematically.

### Eliminate by grammar

Wrong tense?

❌ Remove.

Wrong word form?

❌ Remove.

Wrong preposition?

❌ Remove.

Wrong singular/plural?

❌ Remove.

### Then eliminate by meaning

Doesn't match the sentence?

❌ Remove.

### Then check collocation

Does the phrase sound naturally correct?

Choose the strongest remaining option.

# 14. Worked Examples

## Example 1

> The instructions were extremely ______, so everyone completed the task without assistance.

A. vague
B. ambiguous
C. clear
D. confusing

✅ **Answer: C. clear**

Clue:

> everyone completed the task without assistance

---

## Example 2

> Although the task was difficult, the candidate remained ______.

A. determined
B. discouraged
C. careless
D. confused

✅ **Answer: A. determined**

Clue:

> Although = contrast

Difficulty → expected negative reaction.

But the sentence says the candidate **remained** something despite difficulty.

---

## Example 3

> The manager is responsible _____ training new employees.

A. of
B. to
C. for
D. with

✅ **Answer: C. for**

Collocation:

> responsible for

---

## Example 4

> The company introduced a new system to ______ productivity.

A. increase
B. rise
C. arise
D. raise up

✅ **Answer: A. increase**

Natural expression:

> increase productivity

---

## Example 5 — Double Blank

> The proposal looked ______ on paper, but its implementation was extremely ______.

A. practical / difficult
B. impossible / easy
C. harmful / beneficial
D. unsuccessful / promising

✅ **Answer: A. practical / difficult**

The word **but** creates contrast.

# 15. Top 10 Exam Questions

### Q1. What should you read first?

**The complete sentence.**

---

### Q2. Should you look at options immediately?

Preferably no.

Predict the answer first.

---

### Q3. What are clue words?

Words that reveal the logical relationship between ideas.

Examples:

```text
although → contrast
because → cause
therefore → result
and → addition
```

---

### Q4. What is the biggest mistake?

Choosing a word that fits grammatically but not contextually.

---

### Q5. How should vocabulary be learned?

Learn words **in context and collocations**, not only isolated definitions.

---

### Q6. How should double blanks be solved?

Evaluate both blanks together.

---

### Q7. What if you don't know a word?

Use:

```text
grammar
context
tone
clue words
elimination
```

---

### Q8. What is a collocation?

A natural combination of words.

Example:

> make a decision

---

### Q9. What does "although" usually indicate?

**Contrast.**

---

### Q10. What is the final check?

Read the completed sentence naturally.

# 16. 30-Second Revision

```text
READ THE WHOLE SENTENCE
        ↓
PREDICT THE WORD
        ↓
FIND CLUE WORDS
        ↓
CHECK GRAMMAR
        ↓
CHECK TONE
        ↓
CHECK MEANING
        ↓
CHECK COLLOCATION
        ↓
ELIMINATE OPTIONS
        ↓
RE-READ
```

### 🧠 Golden Rule

> **Grammar tells you what can fit. Context tells you what should fit.**

### Quick Memory

```text
BUT / ALTHOUGH → CONTRAST
BECAUSE → CAUSE
THEREFORE → RESULT
AND / ALSO → ADDITION
DESPITE → CONTRAST
```
