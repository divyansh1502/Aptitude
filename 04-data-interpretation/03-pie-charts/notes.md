# 1. What is a Pie Chart?

A pie chart is a circular graph divided into sectors.

The **entire circle represents the total quantity**.

```text
Entire Circle = 360° = 100% = Total
```

Each sector represents a part of the total.

### Example

If a sector occupies 25% of the circle:

$$
25\% \times 360^\circ = 90^\circ
$$

Therefore, that sector has an angle of **90°**.

---

# 2. Basic Concept

The three most important relationships are:

$$
360^\circ = 100\%
$$

$$
180^\circ = 50\%
$$

$$
90^\circ = 25\%
$$

$$
1^\circ = \frac{100}{360}\%
$$

A sector with angle `θ` represents:

$$
\frac{\theta}{360}\times100
$$

of the total.

---

# 3. Pie Chart Angle

If total quantity is `T` and a category represents `x`:

$$
Angle=\frac{x}{T}\times360^\circ
$$

### Example

Total students = 800

Engineering students = 200

$$
Angle=\frac{200}{800}\times360
$$

$$
=90^\circ
$$

---

# 4. Converting Angle to Value

If:

* Total = `T`
* Sector angle = `θ`

Then:

$$
Value=\frac{\theta}{360}\times T
$$

### Example

Total employees = 720

Sector angle = 120°

$$
Value=\frac{120}{360}\times720
$$

$$
=\frac13\times720
$$

$$
=240
$$

**Answer: 240 employees**

---

# 5. Converting Value to Angle

If:

* Category value = `x`
* Total = `T`

Then:

$$
Angle=\frac{x}{T}\times360^\circ
$$

### Example

Total production = 1000

Product A = 250

$$
Angle=\frac{250}{1000}\times360
$$

$$
=90^\circ
$$

---

# 6. Percentage from Angle

$$
Percentage=\frac{\theta}{360}\times100
$$

### Example

Angle = 72°

$$
\frac{72}{360}\times100
$$

$$
=20\%
$$

So a 72° sector represents **20%**.

---

# 7. Percentage to Angle

$$
Angle=\frac{Percentage}{100}\times360
$$

or:

$$
Angle=Percentage\times3.6
$$

### Example

40%:

$$
40\times3.6=144^\circ
$$

---

# 8. Ratio from Pie Chart

Since every sector has the same total:

$$
Ratio\ of\ values = Ratio\ of\ angles
$$

### Example

A = 60°

B = 120°

$$
A:B=60:120
$$

$$
=1:2
$$

### Important

You can compare sectors directly using their angles.

---

# 9. Common Question Types

| Question Type            | Method                 |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Find value from angle    | Angle ÷ 360 × Total    |
| Find angle from value    | Value ÷ Total × 360    |
| Find percentage          | Angle ÷ 360 × 100      |
| Find ratio               | Compare angles         |
| Find total               | Value ÷ percentage     |
| Find difference          | Subtract values        |
| Find average             | Sum ÷ count            |
| Find combined percentage | Add sector percentages |
| Find combined value      | Add sector values      |
| Compare two sectors      | Ratio/difference       |

# 10. Solved Examples

## Example 1 — Find Value

A pie chart represents the distribution of 900 students.

A sector has an angle of 80°.

Find the number of students.

$$
Value=\frac{80}{360}\times900
$$

$$
=\frac{2}{9}\times900
$$

$$
=200
$$

**Answer: 200 students**

---

## Example 2 — Find Percentage

A sector has an angle of 108°.

$$
Percentage=\frac{108}{360}\times100
$$

$$
=30\%
$$

**Answer: 30%**

---

## Example 3 — Find Angle

A company spends 15% of its budget on advertising.

$$
Angle=15\times3.6
$$

$$
=54^\circ
$$

**Answer: 54°**

---

## Example 4 — Find Ratio

Two sectors are:

* A = 72°
* B = 108°

$$
A:B=72:108
$$

Divide by 36:

$$
=2:3
$$

**Answer: 2:3**

---

## Example 5 — Find Total

A sector represents 25% of a total and contains 150 units.

$$
Total=\frac{150\times100}{25}
$$

$$
=600
$$

**Answer: 600**

# 11. Multiple Pie Charts

Sometimes two or more pie charts are given.

Example:

### 2024

| Category | Percentage |
| -------- | ---------: |
| A        |        30% |
| B        |        20% |
| C        |        25% |
| D        |        25% |

### 2025

| Category | Percentage |
| -------- | ---------: |
| A        |        25% |
| B        |        30% |
| C        |        20% |
| D        |        25% |

The important point is:

> **Percentages can be compared directly only when the totals are equal.**

If totals are different, calculate actual values first.

---

## Example

2024 total = 1000

A = 30%

$$
A=300
$$

2025 total = 2000

A = 25%

$$
A=500
$$

Although A's percentage decreased from 30% to 25%, its actual value increased from 300 to 500.

This is a very common DI trap.

---

# 12. Common Traps

## 🚨 Trap 1 — Forgetting 360°

Always remember:

$$
360^\circ=100\%
$$

---

## 🚨 Trap 2 — Wrong Conversion

Correct:

$$
Value=\frac{Angle}{360}\times Total
$$

Not:

$$
\frac{360}{Angle}\times Total
$$

---

## 🚨 Trap 3 — Comparing Percentages When Totals Differ

A larger percentage does not necessarily mean a larger actual value.

---

## 🚨 Trap 4 — Sector Angle Confusion

If a sector is visually large, don't estimate its angle. Use the given value/angle or calculate it.

---

## 🚨 Trap 5 — Forgetting the Remaining Sector

If all other sectors are known:

$$
Remaining=360^\circ-\sum Known\ Angles
$$

---

## 🚨 Trap 6 — Ratio Order

If asked:

> Ratio of A to B

write:

$$
A:B
$$

not `B:A`.

# 13. Placement Strategy

Use this workflow:

```text
Read Title
    ↓
Identify Total
    ↓
Check Unit
    ↓
Identify Given Information
    ↓
Angle / Percentage / Value
    ↓
Convert if Required
    ↓
Calculate
    ↓
Check Answer
```
