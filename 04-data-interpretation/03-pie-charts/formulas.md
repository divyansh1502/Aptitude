# 1. Core Formula

The complete pie chart represents:

$$
360^\circ=100\%=Total
$$

Therefore:

$$
1\%=3.6^\circ
$$

and:

$$
1^\circ=\frac{1}{3.6}\%
$$

---

# 2. Angle to Value

$$
Value=\frac{\theta}{360}\times Total
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{90}{360}\times800=200
$$

---

# 3. Value to Angle

$$
Angle=\frac{Value}{Total}\times360
$$

Example:

$$
\frac{200}{800}\times360=90^\circ
$$

---

# 4. Angle to Percentage

$$
Percentage=\frac{\theta}{360}\times100
$$

### Shortcut

$$
Percentage=\frac{\theta}{3.6}
$$

Example:

$$
108\div3.6=30\%
$$

---

# 5. Percentage to Angle

$$
Angle=Percentage\times3.6
$$

Example:

$$
25\times3.6=90^\circ
$$

---

# 6. Finding Total

If value and percentage are known:

$$
Total=\frac{Value\times100}{Percentage}
$$

If value and angle are known:

$$
Total=\frac{Value\times360}{Angle}
$$

---

# 7. Ratio

$$
A:B=Angle_A:Angle_B
$$

Example:

$$
72:144=1:2
$$

---

# 8. Remaining Sector

$$
Remaining\ Angle=360^\circ-\sum Known\ Angles
$$

If using percentages:

$$
Remaining\%=100\%-\sum Known\%
$$

---

# 9. Combined Sectors

If A = 20% and B = 30%:

$$
A+B=50\%
$$

Corresponding angle:

$$
50\times3.6=180^\circ
$$

---

# 10. Successive Changes

If the actual values represented by a sector change:

$$
Percentage\ Change=
\frac{New-Old}{Old}\times100
$$

For successive percentage changes:

$$
Net\ Change=a+b+\frac{ab}{100}
$$

Use negative values for decreases.

Example:

+20%, then −10%:

$$
20-10-\frac{20\times10}{100}
$$

$$
=8\%
$$

# 12. Quick Cheat Sheet

```text
Whole Circle
= 360°
= 100%

1%
= 3.6°

Angle → Percentage
= Angle / 360 × 100

Percentage → Angle
= Percentage × 3.6

Angle → Value
= Angle / 360 × Total

Value → Angle
= Value / Total × 360

Value + Percentage → Total
= Value × 100 / Percentage

Value + Angle → Total
= Value × 360 / Angle

Ratio
= Angle₁ : Angle₂

Remaining Angle
= 360° − Known Angles

Remaining %
= 100% − Known %
```

### 🧠 Fastest Rule

> **Angle ÷ 3.6 = Percentage**

and

> **Percentage × 3.6 = Angle**
