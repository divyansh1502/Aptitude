# 1. C — Calculation Mistakes

Common errors:

* Incorrect multiplication
* Incorrect division
* Percentage calculation error
* Ratio simplification error
* Arithmetic mistake

### Prevention

Use:

$$
Value=\frac{Angle}{360}\times Total
$$

Simplify the fraction before multiplying whenever possible.

---

# 2. S — Selection Mistakes

Examples:

* Selecting the wrong sector
* Using the wrong year
* Using the wrong category
* Confusing one pie chart with another

### Prevention

Write the required information before calculating:

```text
Chart:
Year:
Category:
Total:
Given:
Required:
```

---

# 3. T — Interpretation Mistakes

Common pie-chart interpretation errors:

* Forgetting that the full circle is 360°
* Confusing angle with percentage
* Ignoring the total
* Reading 25% as 25°
* Forgetting that 1% = 3.6°

### Prevention

Always write:

$$
360^\circ=100\%
$$

before solving unfamiliar pie-chart questions.

---

# 4. R — Reasoning Mistakes

## Mistake: Comparing Percentages Instead of Actual Values

Example:

Company A:

30% of 1000 = 300

Company B:

25% of 2000 = 500

Although:

$$
30\%>25\%
$$

Company B has the larger actual value.

### Rule

> When totals are different, convert percentages into actual values before comparing.

---

# 5. G — Guess / Time Management Mistakes

Common problems:

* Spending too long on a simple conversion
* Recalculating unnecessarily
* Guessing a sector angle visually
* Not using known angle-percentage conversions

### Useful shortcuts

```text
90° = 25%
180° = 50%
270° = 75%

36° = 10%
72° = 20%
108° = 30%
144° = 40%
```

# 🚨 Most Common Pie-Chart Traps

## Trap 1 — 360° vs 100%

Remember:

$$
360^\circ=100\%
$$

---

## Trap 2 — Wrong Denominator

For percentage change:

$$
\frac{New-Old}{Old}\times100
$$

The denominator is the **old value**.

---

## Trap 3 — Different Totals

Never compare sector percentages directly if the underlying totals differ.

---

## Trap 4 — Remaining Sector

Use:

$$
360^\circ-\text{known angles}
$$

not `100 - angles`.

---

## Trap 5 — Ratio Direction

If the question asks:

> A to B

write:

$$
A:B
$$
