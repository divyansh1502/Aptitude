# 1. What is Table DI?

In Table DI, numerical information is presented in a table containing **rows and columns**.

Example:

| Year | Company A | Company B | Company C |
| ---- | --------: | --------: | --------: |
| 2021 |       120 |       150 |       100 |
| 2022 |       140 |       180 |       120 |
| 2023 |       160 |       200 |       150 |

Questions may ask for:

* Total
* Difference
* Ratio
* Percentage
* Average
* Percentage increase/decrease
* Maximum/minimum
* Combined values
* Missing values

### Core Principle

> **Do not calculate everything in the table. Calculate only what the question requires.**

This is one of the biggest time-saving techniques in DI.

---

# 2. Anatomy of a DI Table

A table generally contains:

### Rows

Rows may represent:

* Years
* Companies
* Cities
* Products
* Students
* Departments

### Columns

Columns may represent:

* Revenue
* Profit
* Sales
* Employees
* Production
* Expenses

Example:

| Year | Revenue | Expense | Profit |
| ---- | ------: | ------: | -----: |
| 2021 |     500 |     300 |    200 |
| 2022 |     600 |     350 |    250 |
| 2023 |     750 |     400 |    350 |

Here:

* Rows → Years
* Columns → Revenue, Expense, Profit
* Numbers → Data values

# 3. How to Read a DI Table

Use the following process.

## Step 1 — Check the units

Look for:

* ₹
* lakh
* crore
* thousand
* million
* percentage
* tonnes
* kilograms
* units

Never ignore the unit.

---

## Step 2 — Understand the rows

Ask:

> What does each row represent?

For example:

```text
Rows → Years
```

or:

```text
Rows → Companies
```

---

## Step 3 — Understand the columns

Ask:

> What does each column represent?

For example:

```text
Columns → Sales, Profit, Employees
```

---

## Step 4 — Read the question

Identify exactly what the question asks.

Example:

> What is the percentage increase in sales from 2022 to 2023?

You only need:

* 2022 sales
* 2023 sales

You do not need the rest of the table.

---

## Step 5 — Select the shortest method

Before calculating, ask:

> Can I simplify the calculation?

Useful techniques:

* Fraction conversion
* Cancellation
* Approximation
* Mental arithmetic
* Ratio simplification

---

# 4. Common Question Types

| Question Type       | Main Operation                 |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Total               | Addition                       |
| Difference          | Subtraction                    |
| Ratio               | Division + simplification      |
| Percentage          | Part ÷ Whole × 100             |
| Average             | Sum ÷ Count                    |
| Increase            | New − Old                      |
| Decrease            | Old − New                      |
| Percentage increase | Increase ÷ Old × 100           |
| Percentage decrease | Decrease ÷ Old × 100           |
| Maximum             | Largest value                  |
| Minimum             | Smallest value                 |
| Combined data       | Selected values added together |
| Missing data        | Use given relationship         |

# 5. Percentage Questions

## Basic Formula

$$
Percentage = \frac{Part}{Whole}\times100
$$

### Example

A company sold 240 units.

60 units were premium products.

$$
Percentage=\frac{60}{240}\times100
$$

$$
=25\%
$$

**Answer: 25%**

---

## Example

| Product | Sales |
| ------- | ----: |
| A       |   200 |
| B       |   300 |
| C       |   500 |

What percentage of total sales comes from B?

Total:

$$
200+300+500=1000
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{300}{1000}\times100=30\%
$$

**Answer: 30%**

---

# 6. Ratio Questions

## Basic Formula

$$
Ratio=A:B
$$

Simplify both terms using their HCF.

### Example

A = 240

B = 360

$$
240:360
$$

Divide by 120:

$$
2:3
$$

**Answer: 2:3**

---

## Ratio Order Matters

If:

$$
A=200,\quad B=300
$$

Then:

$$
A:B=2:3
$$

but:

$$
B:A=3:2
$$

Therefore:

> "Ratio of A to B" means **A:B**.

---

# 7. Average Questions

## Formula

$$
Average=\frac{Sum\ of\ values}{Number\ of\ values}
$$

### Example

| Year | Sales |
| ---- | ----: |
| 2021 |   100 |
| 2022 |   150 |
| 2023 |   200 |

$$
Average=\frac{100+150+200}{3}
$$

$$
=\frac{450}{3}=150
$$

**Answer: 150**

---

## Weighted Average

When groups have different sizes, a simple average may be incorrect.

Example:

* 10 students → average = 80
* 30 students → average = 60

Overall average:

$$
\frac{10(80)+30(60)}{10+30}
$$

$$
=\frac{800+1800}{40}
$$

$$
=65
$$

Therefore, overall average = **65**.

---

# 8. Difference and Comparison

## Absolute Difference

$$
Difference=|A-B|
$$

Example:

A = 750

B = 620

$$
750-620=130
$$

Difference = **130**

---

## How Much More?

If A = 800 and B = 600:

$$
800-600=200
$$

A is **200 more than B**.

---

## How Much Less?

If A = 600 and B = 800:

$$
800-600=200
$$

A is **200 less than B**.

---

# 9. Percentage Increase and Decrease

## Percentage Increase

$$
Percentage\ Increase=
\frac{New-Old}{Old}\times100
$$

Example:

Sales increased from 400 to 500.

$$
Increase=500-400=100
$$

$$
\frac{100}{400}\times100=25\%
$$

**Answer: 25%**

---

## Percentage Decrease

$$
Percentage\ Decrease=
\frac{Old-New}{Old}\times100
$$

Example:

Sales decreased from 500 to 400.

$$
Decrease=500-400=100
$$

$$
\frac{100}{500}\times100=20\%
$$

**Answer: 20%**

---

## 🚨 Golden Rule

For percentage increase/decrease:

> **Original value is the denominator.**

# 11. Missing Data

Sometimes one or more values are unknown.

Example:

| Year | Sales |
| ---- | ----: |
| 2021 |   200 |
| 2022 |     ? |
| 2023 |   400 |

If sales increased by 50% from 2021 to 2022:

$$
2022=200\times1.5
$$

$$
=300
$$

Therefore:

**2022 sales = 300**

---

## Reverse Percentage

If the value after a 25% increase is 500:

$$
New=Old\times1.25
$$

Therefore:

$$
Old=\frac{500}{1.25}=400
$$

---

# 12. Multi-Column Tables

Example:

| Company | Revenue | Expense | Profit |
| ------- | ------: | ------: | -----: |
| A       |     500 |     300 |    200 |
| B       |     600 |     350 |    250 |
| C       |     700 |     400 |    300 |

Question:

> What is the total revenue of A and C?

Only use the Revenue column:

$$
500+700=1200
$$

**Answer: 1200**

---

## Combined Percentage

| Company | Male | Female |
| ------- | ---: | -----: |
| A       |   60 |     40 |
| B       |   80 |     20 |

Total females:

$$
40+20=60
$$

Total employees:

$$
60+40+80+20=200
$$

Percentage:

$$
\frac{60}{200}\times100=30\%
$$

**Answer: 30%**

# 13. Calculation Shortcuts

## 13.1 Important Fractions

| Fraction | Percentage |
| -------: | ---------: |
|      1/2 |        50% |
|      1/3 |     33.33% |
|      1/4 |        25% |
|      1/5 |        20% |
|      1/6 |     16.67% |
|      1/8 |      12.5% |
|     1/10 |        10% |
|     1/20 |         5% |
|     1/25 |         4% |
|     1/50 |         2% |

---

## 13.2 Percentage Multipliers

| Change | Multiplier |
| -----: | ---------: |
|   +10% |      ×1.10 |
|   +20% |      ×1.20 |
|   +25% |      ×1.25 |
|   +50% |      ×1.50 |
|  +100% |         ×2 |
|   −10% |      ×0.90 |
|   −20% |      ×0.80 |
|   −25% |      ×0.75 |
|   −50% |      ×0.50 |

---

## 13.3 Cancel Before Multiplying

Instead of:

$$
\frac{240}{600}\times100
$$

Simplify:

$$
\frac{240}{600}=\frac{2}{5}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{2}{5}\times100=40\%
$$

---

## 13.4 Estimate When Possible

If options are:

* 22%
* 35%
* 48%
* 65%

and your rough calculation gives approximately 47%, option 48% can be selected.

Use exact calculations when options are close.

# 15. Common Traps

### 🚨 Trap 1 — Wrong denominator

For percentage increase:

$$
\frac{Increase}{Original}\times100
$$

Not:

$$
\frac{Increase}{New}\times100
$$

---

### 🚨 Trap 2 — Wrong ratio order

"Ratio of A to B" means:

$$
A:B
$$

---

### 🚨 Trap 3 — Ignoring units

If revenue is given in ₹ crore, keep calculations in crore unless conversion is required.

---

### 🚨 Trap 4 — Unnecessary calculations

Don't calculate the entire table when only two cells are required.

---

### 🚨 Trap 5 — Percentage vs percentage points

If a value changes from 40% to 50%:

Percentage-point increase:

$$
50-40=10
$$

Percentage increase:

$$
\frac{50-40}{40}\times100=25\%
$$

---

### 🚨 Trap 6 — Incorrect simple average

When group sizes differ, check whether weighted average is required.

# 16. Placement Strategy

Use this workflow:

```text
Read the table
      ↓
Check units
      ↓
Understand rows & columns
      ↓
Read the question
      ↓
Identify required cells
      ↓
Choose shortest calculation
      ↓
Calculate
      ↓
Check answer
```

### Speed Rules

1. Don't calculate unnecessary values.
2. Memorize common fractions.
3. Cancel before multiplying.
4. Use approximation when options permit.
5. Be careful with percentage denominators.
6. Check units.
7. Keep rough work organized.
8. Verify the final answer if time permits.
