# 1. What is a Caselet?

A caselet is a short passage containing numerical information.

Unlike a table or graph, the data is not already organized.

### Example

> A company has 800 employees. 40% work in development, 25% work in testing, 15% work in HR, and the remaining employees work in administration.

The questions may ask:

* Number of developers
* Number of testers
* Number of HR employees
* Number of administrators
* Ratio of developers to testers
* Difference between departments

The first task is to **extract the information**.

---

# 2. Why Caselets Are Difficult

Caselets usually combine:

* Reading comprehension
* Data extraction
* Percentage
* Ratio
* Average
* Arithmetic
* Multi-step reasoning

The calculation itself may be easy.

The difficulty is often identifying **which numbers should be used**.

### Golden Rule

> **Do not calculate directly from the paragraph. First convert the paragraph into structured data.**

# 3. Caselet Solving Framework

Use:

```text
READ
  ↓
EXTRACT
  ↓
ORGANIZE
  ↓
IDENTIFY
  ↓
CALCULATE
  ↓
VERIFY
```

### Step 1 — Read the caselet

Understand the context.

### Step 2 — Extract numbers

Underline or note:

* Totals
* Percentages
* Ratios
* Averages
* Years
* Differences

### Step 3 — Organize

Create a mini-table.

### Step 4 — Identify the question

Determine exactly what is required.

### Step 5 — Calculate

Use the required formula only.

### Step 6 — Verify

Check whether the answer makes sense.

# 4. Extracting Data

Suppose:

> A college has 1200 students. 45% are from CSE, 30% from ECE, and 15% from Mechanical. The remaining students belong to other branches.

Extract:

| Branch     | Percentage |
| ---------- | ---------: |
| CSE        |        45% |
| ECE        |        30% |
| Mechanical |        15% |
| Others     |        10% |

Because:

$$
100-(45+30+15)=10\%
$$

Now the paragraph has become a simple table.

---

# 5. Building a Quick Table

For a caselet, quickly create:

| Category | Value |  % | Ratio |
| -------- | ----: | -: | ----: |
| A        |       |    |       |
| B        |       |    |       |
| C        |       |    |       |
| D        |       |    |       |

Fill only the information required.

### Example

> Total = 1000
> A = 40%
> B = 30%
> C = 20%
> Remaining = D

Immediately write:

| Category |   % | Value |
| -------- | --: | ----: |
| A        | 40% |   400 |
| B        | 30% |   300 |
| C        | 20% |   200 |
| D        | 10% |   100 |

# 6. Common Question Types

| Question            | Operation                   |
| ------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Total               | Addition                    |
| Difference          | Subtraction                 |
| Ratio               | Division + simplification   |
| Average             | Sum ÷ count                 |
| Percentage          | Part ÷ whole × 100          |
| Percentage increase | Increase ÷ original × 100   |
| Percentage decrease | Decrease ÷ original × 100   |
| Remaining value     | Total − known values        |
| Combined percentage | Add percentages             |
| Weighted average    | Weighted sum ÷ total weight |

# 7. Percentage-Based Caselets

### Formula

$$
Part=\frac{Percentage}{100}\times Total
$$

### Example

Total employees = 800

Development = 40%

$$
Development=\frac{40}{100}\times800
$$

$$
=320
$$

---

## Remaining Percentage

If:

* A = 40%
* B = 25%
* C = 15%

Then:

$$
Remaining=100-(40+25+15)
$$

$$
=20\%
$$

---

# 8. Ratio-Based Caselets

Suppose:

> The number of boys and girls is in the ratio 3:2. There are 500 students in total.

Total ratio parts:

$$
3+2=5
$$

One part:

$$
500/5=100
$$

Boys:

$$
3\times100=300
$$

Girls:

$$
2\times100=200
$$

---

# 9. Average-Based Caselets

### Formula

$$
Average=\frac{Total}{Number}
$$

If average and number are known:

$$
Total=Average\times Number
$$

### Example

Average sales of 5 months = 240.

Total sales:

$$
240\times5=1200
$$

---

# 10. Difference and Comparison

### Absolute Difference

$$
|A-B|
$$

### Percentage Difference / Increase

When comparing change from an original value:

$$
\frac{New-Old}{Old}\times100
$$

### Example

Production increases from 400 to 500.

Difference:

$$
100
$$

Percentage increase:

$$
\frac{100}{400}\times100=25\%
$$

---

# 11. Multi-Step Caselets

These questions require two or more calculations.

### Example

> A company has 2000 employees. 40% work in technical departments. Of the technical employees, 25% are developers.

First:

$$
Technical=40\%\times2000=800
$$

Then:

$$
Developers=25\%\times800
$$

$$
=200
$$

### Important

The second percentage is applied to **technical employees**, not total employees.

# 12. Solved Examples

## Example 1 — Percentage Caselet

> A company has 1500 employees. 40% work in development, 20% in testing, 15% in HR, and the remaining employees work in operations.

### Development

$$
40\%\times1500=600
$$

### Testing

$$
20\%\times1500=300
$$

### HR

$$
15\%\times1500=225
$$

### Operations

$$
1500-(600+300+225)
$$

$$
=375
$$

### Ratio of Development to Operations

$$
600:375
$$

Divide by 75:

$$
8:5
$$

---

## Example 2 — Ratio Caselet

> A school has 900 students. The ratio of boys to girls is 4:5.

Total parts:

$$
4+5=9
$$

One part:

$$
900/9=100
$$

Boys:

$$
4\times100=400
$$

Girls:

$$
5\times100=500
$$

Difference:

$$
500-400=100
$$

---

## Example 3 — Multi-Step Caselet

> A company has 2400 employees. 50% are technical employees. Among technical employees, 60% are developers and the remaining are testers.

Technical:

$$
50\%\times2400=1200
$$

Developers:

$$
60\%\times1200=720
$$

Testers:

$$
1200-720=480
$$

Ratio:

$$
720:480=3:2
$$

---

## Example 4 — Average Caselet

> The average monthly sales of a company for 6 months is 450 units. During the first five months, sales were 400, 420, 460, 480 and 440 units.

Total six-month sales:

$$
450\times6=2700
$$

First five months:

$$
400+420+460+480+440=2200
$$

Sixth month:

$$
2700-2200=500
$$

**Answer: 500 units**

---

# 13. Common Traps

## 🚨 Trap 1 — Wrong Base

> 20% of technical employees

does **not** mean 20% of total employees.

Always identify the base.

---

## 🚨 Trap 2 — Remaining Value

If categories are given as percentages:

$$
Remaining=100-\text{known percentages}
$$

If values are given:

$$
Remaining=Total-\text{known values}
$$

---

## 🚨 Trap 3 — Ratio Parts

For ratio:

$$
a:b
$$

total parts are:

$$
a+b
$$

---

## 🚨 Trap 4 — Percentage Change

Use the original value as the denominator.

---

## 🚨 Trap 5 — Unnecessary Calculations

Do not calculate every number in the caselet.

Calculate only what the question needs.

---

## 🚨 Trap 6 — Missing Units

Watch for:

* ₹
* thousand
* lakh
* crore
* million
* kg
* tonnes
* students
* employees

---

## 🚨 Trap 7 — Multi-Step Percentage

If:

> 30% are managers and 20% of managers are women

then:

$$
Women=20\%\times30\%\times Total
$$

not simply 20% of total.

# 14. Placement Strategy

## The 5-Second Extraction Method

When reading the caselet, identify:

**T → Total**

**P → Percentage**

**R → Ratio**

**A → Average**

**Q → Question requirement**

Then solve.

---

## Mini-Table Strategy

Never repeatedly reread a long caselet.

Convert it once:

```text
Total = 2000

A = 40% = 800
B = 25% = 500
C = 15% = 300
D = 20% = 400
```

Then answer all questions from this structure.

---

## Question-First Strategy

For a long caselet:

1. Read the question.
2. Identify required variables.
3. Scan the paragraph for those variables.
4. Calculate only what is needed.

This saves time.

# 15. Quick Revision

### Percentage

$$
Part=\frac{P}{100}\timesTotal
$$

### Remaining

$$
Remaining=Total-Known
$$

### Ratio

$$
One\ Part=\frac{Total}{Sum\ of\ Ratio}
$$

### Average

$$
Average=\frac{Sum}{n}
$$

### Total from Average

$$
Total=Average\times n
$$

### Percentage Change

$$
\frac{New-Old}{Old}\times100
$$

### Caselet Golden Rule

> **Paragraph → Mini-table → Formula → Answer**

### 🧠 Memory Trick

**E-O-C-V**

**E → Extract**

**O → Organize**

**C → Calculate**

**V → Verify**

This is the core workflow for caselet DI.
