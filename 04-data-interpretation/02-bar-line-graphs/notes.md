# 1. What is Graph DI?

Graph DI presents numerical information visually instead of using a traditional table.

The most common graph types are:

* Bar graphs
* Double bar graphs
* Multiple bar graphs
* Line graphs

The questions usually involve:

* Total
* Difference
* Ratio
* Average
* Percentage
* Percentage increase/decrease
* Maximum/minimum
* Trends
* Combined values

### Core Principle

> **Read the graph first, calculate second.**

Do not start calculating before understanding the axis, scale, unit, and categories.

---

# 2. Bar Graph

A bar graph represents data using rectangular bars.

Example:

```text
Sales

500 |             █
400 |       █     █
300 | █     █     █
200 | █     █     █     █
100 | █     █     █     █
    +---------------------
       A     B     C     D
```

The height of each bar represents its value.

---

## Types of Bar Graphs

### Single Bar Graph

One value is represented for each category.

Example:

```text
Company → Revenue
A → 200
B → 300
C → 450
D → 350
```

---

### Double Bar Graph

Two related values are shown for each category.

Example:

```text
Company → 2024 Sales and 2025 Sales
```

Useful for comparison.

---

### Multiple Bar Graph

More than two values are shown for each category.

Example:

```text
Company → Sales, Profit, Expenses
```

These graphs require careful identification of each bar.

---

# 3. Line Graph

A line graph represents data using points connected by lines.

It is particularly useful for showing **change over time**.

Example:

| Year | Sales |
| ---- | ----: |
| 2021 |   100 |
| 2022 |   150 |
| 2023 |   130 |
| 2024 |   200 |

The line allows you to quickly observe:

* Increase
* Decrease
* Highest point
* Lowest point
* Overall trend
* Fluctuation

---

# 4. Reading a Graph

Before solving any question, check these five things.

## 1. Title

Understand what the graph represents.

Example:

> Annual production of five factories

---

## 2. X-axis

Usually represents:

* Years
* Companies
* Products
* Cities
* Categories

---

## 3. Y-axis

Usually represents the numerical quantity.

Examples:

* Sales
* Revenue
* Students
* Production
* Population

---

## 4. Scale

This is extremely important.

For example:

```text
1 unit on graph = 50 students
```

If the bar reaches 8 units:

$$
8\times50=400
$$

Therefore the actual value is **400 students**.

---

## 5. Unit

Check whether the values are:

* Units
* Thousands
* Lakhs
* Crores
* Millions
* Percentage
* Kilograms
* Tonnes

---

# 5. Single Bar Graph

Consider:

| Company | Sales |
| ------- | ----: |
| A       |   200 |
| B       |   350 |
| C       |   500 |
| D       |   400 |

### Example

Which company has the highest sales?

Answer:

**Company C**

---

### Total Sales

$$
200+350+500+400=1450
$$

---

### Average Sales

$$
\frac{1450}{4}=362.5
$$

---

### Difference Between C and A

$$
500-200=300
$$

---

# 6. Double Bar Graph

A double bar graph compares two values for every category.

Example:

| Company | 2024 | 2025 |
| ------- | ---: | ---: |
| A       |  200 |  250 |
| B       |  300 |  360 |
| C       |  400 |  500 |

Questions may ask:

* Difference between years
* Total for both years
* Percentage increase
* Ratio between two years
* Company with maximum growth

---

## Example

For Company B:

2024 = 300

2025 = 360

Increase:

$$
360-300=60
$$

Percentage increase:

$$
\frac{60}{300}\times100=20\%
$$

---

# 7. Multiple Bar Graph

Multiple bar graphs show several values for each category.

Example:

| Company | Revenue | Expense | Profit |
| ------- | ------: | ------: | -----: |
| A       |     500 |     350 |    150 |
| B       |     700 |     450 |    250 |
| C       |     800 |     500 |    300 |

Always identify the correct bar before calculating.

### Common mistake

Using the Revenue bar when the question asks about Profit.

---

# 8. Line Graph

Line graphs are especially useful for time-based data.

Example:

| Year | Production |
| ---- | ---------: |
| 2020 |        100 |
| 2021 |        120 |
| 2022 |        150 |
| 2023 |        130 |
| 2024 |        180 |

From this graph:

* Highest → 2024
* Lowest → 2020
* Increase from 2020 to 2024 → 80
* Decrease from 2022 to 2023 → 20

---

## Trend

A trend describes the general movement of data.

### Increasing Trend

Values generally move upward.

### Decreasing Trend

Values generally move downward.

### Fluctuating Trend

Values repeatedly increase and decrease.

# 9. Common Question Types

| Question            | Required Operation        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Total               | Addition                  |
| Difference          | Subtraction               |
| Ratio               | Division + simplification |
| Average             | Sum ÷ count               |
| Percentage          | Part ÷ whole × 100        |
| Percentage increase | Increase ÷ old × 100      |
| Percentage decrease | Decrease ÷ old × 100      |
| Maximum             | Highest value             |
| Minimum             | Lowest value              |
| Growth              | Compare old and new       |
| Trend               | Observe movement          |
| Combined value      | Add selected values       |

# 10. Percentage Questions

## Percentage of Total

$$
Percentage=\frac{Part}{Total}\times100
$$

### Example

Sales of A = 250

Total sales = 1000

$$
\frac{250}{1000}\times100=25\%
$$

---

## Percentage Increase

$$
\frac{New-Old}{Old}\times100
$$

Example:

Sales increase from 400 to 500.

$$
\frac{100}{400}\times100=25\%
$$

---

# 11. Ratio Questions

If:

A = 300

B = 450

Then:

$$
A:B=300:450
$$

Divide by 150:

$$
2:3
$$

### Important

Always maintain the order mentioned in the question.

> Ratio of A to B = A:B

---

# 12. Average Questions

$$
Average=\frac{Sum}{Number\ of\ values}
$$

Example:

Sales:

```text
100, 150, 200, 250
```

$$
Average=\frac{100+150+200+250}{4}
$$

$$
=\frac{700}{4}=175
$$

---

# 13. Trend and Change

## Absolute Change

$$
Change=New-Old
$$

## Percentage Change

$$
\frac{New-Old}{Old}\times100
$$

---

## Example

Production:

2021 = 200

2022 = 250

2023 = 300

2024 = 240

### 2021 → 2024

Absolute increase:

$$
240-200=40
$$

Percentage increase:

$$
\frac{40}{200}\times100=20\%
$$

---

## Highest Growth

Suppose:

| Company | Old | New |
| ------- | --: | --: |
| A       | 100 | 150 |
| B       | 200 | 260 |
| C       | 400 | 500 |

Percentage growth:

A:

$$
\frac{50}{100}\times100=50\%
$$

B:

$$
\frac{60}{200}\times100=30\%
$$

C:

$$
\frac{100}{400}\times100=25\%
$$

Therefore A has the highest percentage growth.

> **Do not confuse highest absolute increase with highest percentage increase.**

# 14. Solved Examples

## Example 1 — Bar Graph

A bar graph gives the following sales:

| Product | Sales |
| ------- | ----: |
| A       |   200 |
| B       |   300 |
| C       |   450 |
| D       |   350 |

### Q1. Find total sales.

$$
200+300+450+350=1300
$$

**Answer: 1300**

### Q2. Find average sales.

$$
\frac{1300}{4}=325
$$

**Answer: 325**

---

## Example 2 — Double Bar Graph

| Company | 2023 | 2024 |
| ------- | ---: | ---: |
| A       |  200 |  240 |
| B       |  300 |  360 |
| C       |  400 |  500 |

### Percentage increase for C

$$
\frac{500-400}{400}\times100
$$

$$
=25\%
$$

**Answer: 25%**

---

## Example 3 — Line Graph

| Year | Revenue |
| ---- | ------: |
| 2020 |     100 |
| 2021 |     150 |
| 2022 |     200 |
| 2023 |     180 |
| 2024 |     250 |

### Highest revenue

2024 = **250**

### Decrease from 2022 to 2023

$$
200-180=20
$$

### Percentage decrease

$$
\frac{20}{200}\times100=10\%
$$

**Answer: 10%**

Suppose:

> 1 cm on the graph represents 200 units.

A bar reaches 6 cm.

$$
6\times200=1200

# 15. Common Traps

## 🚨 Trap 1 — Ignoring the Scale

If one graph unit represents 50 units, do not treat one graph unit as 1.

---

## 🚨 Trap 2 — Wrong Year

In double or multiple graphs, carefully identify the year/category.

---

## 🚨 Trap 3 — Wrong Bar

Multiple bars may represent:

* Revenue
* Profit
* Expense

Always identify the legend.

---

## 🚨 Trap 4 — Wrong Percentage Denominator

Percentage change uses the **old/original value**.

---

## 🚨 Trap 5 — Absolute vs Percentage Growth

A larger numerical increase does not necessarily mean a larger percentage increase.

---

## 🚨 Trap 6 — Misreading the Axis

Check whether the axis starts at:

```text
0
```

or another value.

---

## 🚨 Trap 7 — Assuming a Visual Relationship

Do not estimate values simply because two bars look similar.

Read the actual scale.

---

# 16. Placement Strategy

Use this workflow:

```text
Read title
   ↓
Check X-axis
   ↓
Check Y-axis
   ↓
Check scale
   ↓
Check units / legend
   ↓
Read question
   ↓
Extract required values
   ↓
Calculate
   ↓
Verify
```

### Speed Rules

1. Understand the graph before calculating.
2. Memorize common percentage conversions.
3. Use the graph scale correctly.
4. Don't calculate unused values.
5. For line graphs, compare the exact years requested.
6. Distinguish absolute and percentage growth.
7. Use approximation only when answer choices allow it.

Best for:

> Comparing categories.

### Line Graph

> Showing change or trends over time.

$$
Percentage=\frac{Part}{Whole}\times100
$$

$$
Percentage\ Increase=
\frac{New-Old}{Old}\times100
$$

$$
Percentage\ Decrease=
\frac{Old-New}{Old}\times100
$$

$$
Average=\frac{Sum}{Number}
$$

$$
Difference=|A-B|
$$

### 🧠 Graph DI Memory Trick

**T-S-U-Q-C**

**T → Title**

**S → Scale**

**U → Unit**

**Q → Question**

**C → Calculate**

> **Never calculate before checking the scale and unit.**
