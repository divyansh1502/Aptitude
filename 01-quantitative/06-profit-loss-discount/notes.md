# Profit, Loss & Discount — Notes

## 1. Basic Terms

### Cost Price (CP)

The price at which an item is **purchased**.

```text
CP = Cost Price
```

### Selling Price (SP)

The price at which an item is **sold**.

```text
SP = Selling Price
```

### Marked Price (MP)

The price printed/tagged on the item before discount.

```text
MP = Marked Price
```

### Profit

When:

```text
SP > CP
```

then:

```text
Profit = SP - CP
```

### Loss

When:

```text
CP > SP
```

then:

```text
Loss = CP - SP
```

---

# 2. Profit Percentage

Profit percentage is calculated on **Cost Price**.

```text
Profit% = (Profit/CP) × 100
```

Since:

```text
Profit = SP - CP
```

we get:

```text
Profit%
= ((SP-CP)/CP) × 100
```

### Example

An item is bought for `₹500` and sold for `₹600`.

```text
Profit = 600 - 500
       = ₹100
```

Therefore:

```text
Profit% = (100/500) × 100
        = 20%
```

**Answer: `20% profit`**

---

# 3. Loss Percentage

Loss percentage is calculated on **Cost Price**.

```text
Loss% = (Loss/CP) × 100
```

### Example

An item is bought for `₹800` and sold for `₹680`.

```text
Loss = 800 - 680
     = ₹120
```

Therefore:

```text
Loss% = (120/800) × 100
      = 15%
```

**Answer: `15% loss`**

---

# 4. Finding SP When Profit Percentage is Given

If an item is sold at `p%` profit:

```text
SP = CP × (100+p)/100
```

### Example

CP = `₹500`

Profit = `20%`

```text
SP = 500 × 120/100
   = ₹600
```

**Answer: `₹600`**

---

# 5. Finding SP When Loss Percentage is Given

If an item is sold at `p%` loss:

```text
SP = CP × (100-p)/100
```

### Example

CP = `₹800`

Loss = `15%`

```text
SP = 800 × 85/100
   = ₹680
```

**Answer: `₹680`**

---

# 6. Finding CP from SP and Profit %

Suppose an item is sold for `₹720` at `20%` profit.

```text
SP = CP × 120/100
```

Therefore:

```text
CP = 720 × 100/120
   = ₹600
```

**Answer: `₹600`**

### Shortcut

For `p%` profit:

```text
CP = SP × 100/(100+p)
```

---

# 7. Finding CP from SP and Loss %

Suppose an item is sold for `₹680` at `15%` loss.

```text
SP = CP × 85/100
```

Therefore:

```text
CP = 680 × 100/85
   = ₹800
```

**Answer: `₹800`**

### Shortcut

For `p%` loss:

```text
CP = SP × 100/(100-p)
```

---

# 8. Profit/Loss Using Ratios

If an item is sold at `20%` profit:

```text
CP : SP
= 100 : 120
= 5 : 6
```

If an item is sold at `20%` loss:

```text
CP : SP
= 100 : 80
= 5 : 4
```

This ratio method is extremely useful in exam questions.

---

# 9. Marked Price

Marked Price is the price displayed before discount.

Suppose:

```text
MP = ₹1000
```

and the shop gives a discount.

The customer does not necessarily pay `₹1000`.

The final selling price is calculated after discount.

---

# 10. Discount

Discount is the reduction from Marked Price.

```text
Discount = MP - SP
```

Discount percentage:

```text
Discount%
= (Discount/MP) × 100
```

### Example

MP = `₹2000`

SP = `₹1700`

```text
Discount = 2000 - 1700
         = ₹300
```

Therefore:

```text
Discount%
= (300/2000) × 100
= 15%
```

**Answer: `15% discount`**

---

# 11. Finding SP After Discount

If discount is `d%`:

```text
SP = MP × (100-d)/100
```

### Example

MP = `₹1500`

Discount = `20%`

```text
SP = 1500 × 80/100
   = ₹1200
```

**Answer: `₹1200`**

---

# 12. Finding MP from SP and Discount %

Suppose the selling price after a `20%` discount is `₹1200`.

```text
SP = MP × 80/100
```

Therefore:

```text
MP = 1200 × 100/80
   = ₹1500
```

**Answer: `₹1500`**

---

# 13. Profit After Discount

This is one of the most important placement patterns.

Suppose:

```text
CP = ₹800
MP = ₹1000
Discount = 10%
```

First calculate SP:

```text
SP = 1000 × 90/100
   = ₹900
```

Now compare SP with CP:

```text
Profit = 900 - 800
       = ₹100
```

Profit percentage:

```text
Profit% = (100/800) × 100
        = 12.5%
```

Therefore, the shop gives a `10%` discount but still earns a `12.5%` profit.

### Important

**Discount percentage is calculated on MP.**

**Profit percentage is calculated on CP.**

---

# 14. Marked Price Required for a Desired Profit

Suppose:

```text
CP = ₹800
Desired profit = 20%
Discount = 10%
```

First find required SP:

```text
SP = 800 × 120/100
   = ₹960
```

Now:

```text
SP = MP × 90/100
```

Therefore:

```text
MP = 960 × 100/90
   = ₹1066.67
```

So the item must be marked at approximately:

```text
₹1066.67
```

---

# 15. Discount Required for a Given SP

Suppose:

```text
MP = ₹2000
SP = ₹1700
```

Discount:

```text
2000 - 1700
= ₹300
```

Discount percentage:

```text
300/2000 × 100
= 15%
```

---

# 16. Successive Discounts

Suppose an item has two successive discounts:

```text
20% and 10%
```

Do **not** simply say:

```text
20% + 10% = 30%
```

The second discount is applied after the first discount.

Take MP = `₹100`.

After 20% discount:

```text
100 × 80/100
= ₹80
```

After 10% discount:

```text
80 × 90/100
= ₹72
```

Final price = `₹72`.

Total discount:

```text
100 - 72
= ₹28
```

Therefore effective discount:

```text
28%
```

---

# 17. Successive Discount Formula

For discounts `a%` and `b%`:

```text
Effective Discount
= a + b - ab/100
```

For `20%` and `10%`:

```text
= 20 + 10 - (20×10)/100
= 30 - 2
= 28%
```

**Answer: `28%`**

---

# 18. Three Successive Discounts

For discounts `a%`, `b%`, and `c%`:

Use multiplication:

```text
Final Price
= MP × (1-a/100) × (1-b/100) × (1-c/100)
```

This is safer than trying to remember a long expanded formula.

### Example

Discounts:

```text
10%, 20%, 10%
```

Take MP = `₹100`.

```text
100 × 0.90 × 0.80 × 0.90
= ₹64.80
```

Effective discount:

```text
100 - 64.80
= 35.20%
```

---

# 19. Equal Discounts

If two discounts are both `d%`:

```text
Effective Discount
= 2d - d²/100
```

For two `20%` discounts:

```text
= 40 - 400/100
= 36%
```

Not `40%`.

---

# 20. Profit and Discount Combined

Suppose an item is marked `25%` above its cost price and then a `10%` discount is given.

Take:

```text
CP = ₹100
```

Marked price:

```text
MP = 100 × 125/100
   = ₹125
```

After 10% discount:

```text
SP = 125 × 90/100
   = ₹112.50
```

Profit:

```text
112.50 - 100
= ₹12.50
```

Therefore:

```text
Profit% = 12.5%
```

---

# 21. Shortcut: MP is p% Above CP

If MP is `p%` above CP and discount is `d%`:

Take:

```text
CP = 100
```

Then:

```text
MP = 100(1+p/100)
```

After discount:

```text
SP = 100(1+p/100)(1-d/100)
```

Compare SP with 100 to find profit/loss percentage.

---

# 22. When SP = CP

If:

```text
SP = CP
```

then:

```text
Profit = 0
Loss = 0
```

Therefore:

```text
No Profit, No Loss
```

---

# 23. Same Selling Price: One Profit, One Loss

Suppose two articles are sold for the same price.

One gives `20%` profit and the other gives `20%` loss.

Take SP of each as `₹120`.

For the first article:

```text
CP = 120 × 100/120
   = ₹100
```

For the second:

```text
CP = 120 × 100/80
   = ₹150
```

Total CP:

```text
100 + 150
= ₹250
```

Total SP:

```text
120 + 120
= ₹240
```

Loss:

```text
250 - 240
= ₹10
```

Loss percentage:

```text
10/250 × 100
= 4%
```

### Important Result

If two articles are sold at the **same SP**, one at `x%` profit and one at `x%` loss:

```text
Overall Loss%
= x²/(100)
```

For `20%`:

```text
20²/100
= 4%
```

---

# 24. Same Cost Price: One Profit, One Loss

Suppose two articles have the same CP.

One is sold at `20%` profit and another at `20%` loss.

Take each CP as `₹100`.

First SP:

```text
₹120
```

Second SP:

```text
₹80
```

Total CP:

```text
₹200
```

Total SP:

```text
₹200
```

Therefore:

```text
No profit, no loss
```

### Important

Equal CP + equal percentage profit/loss → **no overall profit or loss**.

---

# 25. Dishonest Dealer — False Weight

A dealer claims to sell `1 kg` but actually gives only `900 g`.

Suppose the dealer charges the normal price of `1 kg`.

The customer pays for:

```text
1000 g
```

but receives:

```text
900 g
```

Gain due to short weight:

```text
1000/900 - 1
= 1/9
```

Percentage gain:

```text
(1/9) × 100
= 11.11%
```

This type of question combines quantity and profit.

---

# 26. Profit When Weight is Less Than Claimed

If a dealer gives `w` units instead of `100` units while charging for `100` units:

```text
Gain%
= (100-w)/w × 100
```

Example: gives `80 g` while charging for `100 g`.

```text
Gain%
= (100-80)/80 × 100
= 25%
```

---

# 27. Loss When Selling Price is a Percentage of CP

Suppose:

```text
SP = 80% of CP
```

Then:

```text
Loss = 20% of CP
```

Therefore:

```text
Loss% = 20%
```

Similarly:

```text
SP = 125% of CP
```

means:

```text
Profit% = 25%
```

---

# 28. Percentage Change in CP and SP

Suppose CP increases but SP remains fixed.

The profit percentage will change because profit percentage is calculated on CP.

### Example

Original:

```text
CP = ₹100
SP = ₹120
```

Profit:

```text
20%
```

If CP becomes `₹110` while SP remains `₹120`:

```text
Profit = ₹10
```

New profit percentage:

```text
10/110 × 100
≈ 9.09%
```

---

# 29. Selling Price for No Profit No Loss

If CP is `₹750` and the seller wants no profit and no loss:

```text
SP = CP
   = ₹750
```

If a discount is offered, MP must be higher than CP.

# 31. The Most Important Distinction

Remember this permanently:

```text
PROFIT / LOSS %
        ↓
Calculated on CP

DISCOUNT %
        ↓
Calculated on MP
```

This single distinction prevents many exam mistakes.

---

# 32. Exam Strategy

When solving a question:

### Step 1

Identify:

```text
CP
SP
MP
```

### Step 2

Determine what percentage is being discussed.

```text
Profit/Loss → CP
Discount → MP
```

### Step 3

If discount is involved:

```text
MP → Discount → SP
```

### Step 4

Then compare:

```text
SP with CP
```

### Step 5

For complicated questions, assume:

```text
CP = ₹100
```

if no actual value is given.

This often makes percentage calculations much faster.

---

# Quick Memory

```text
CP → COST

MP → TAGGED PRICE

DISCOUNT → MP - SP

PROFIT → SP - CP

LOSS → CP - SP

PROFIT% → PROFIT/CP × 100

LOSS% → LOSS/CP × 100

DISCOUNT% → DISCOUNT/MP × 100
```
