## 1. Mixture Basics

A **mixture** is formed when two or more substances are combined.

Examples:

* Milk + Water
* Alcohol + Water
* Acid + Water
* Two solutions with different concentrations
* Rice of different prices
* Tea of different qualities

A mixture can be described using:

* Quantity
* Concentration
* Percentage
* Price per unit
* Ratio
* Quality

### Example

10 L milk + 5 L water:

Total mixture:

```text
10 + 5 = 15 L
```

Milk percentage:

```text
10/15 × 100 = 66.67%
```

Water percentage:

```text
5/15 × 100 = 33.33%
```

---

## 2. Concentration

Concentration tells us how much of the required substance is present in the total mixture.

### Formula

```text
Concentration % = Quantity of solute / Total mixture × 100
```

### Example

A 40 L solution contains 12 L acid.

```text
Acid % = 12/40 × 100
       = 30%
```

Therefore, the solution is **30% acid**.

### Finding solute quantity

```text
Solute = Total mixture × Concentration/100
```

Example:

60 L of a 25% salt solution.

```text
Salt = 60 × 25/100
     = 15 L
```

---

## 3. Weighted Average

When quantities are different, the ordinary average is generally wrong.

Use:

```text
Weighted Average = (q₁v₁ + q₂v₂) / (q₁ + q₂)
```

Where:

* `q` = quantity
* `v` = value/concentration/price

### Example

20 kg rice at ₹40/kg is mixed with 30 kg rice at ₹60/kg.

```text
Average price
= (20×40 + 30×60)/(20+30)
= (800 + 1800)/50
= ₹52/kg
```

So the mixture costs **₹52/kg**.

### Important

Do **not** use:

```text
(40 + 60)/2 = ₹50
```

because the quantities are different.

## 4. Alligation

**Alligation** is a shortcut for finding the ratio in which two quantities having different values should be mixed to obtain a desired mean value.

It is especially useful for:

* Price mixtures
* Concentration mixtures
* Milk-water problems
* Profit-based mixtures

### Formula

If:

```text
Lower value = L
Higher value = H
Mean value = M
```

Then:

```text
Quantity of lower : Quantity of higher

= (H - M) : (M - L)
```

### Example

Rice costing ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg are mixed to obtain a mixture costing ₹48/kg.

```text
Lower = 40
Higher = 60
Mean = 48
```

Therefore:

```text
Cheaper : Dearer
= (60 - 48) : (48 - 40)
= 12 : 8
= 3 : 2
```

So the ratio is:

```text
3 : 2
```

---

## 5. Alligation Diagram

The standard alligation structure is:

```text
        Lower value
             \ 
              \  H - M
               \
                M
               /
              /  M - L
             /
        Higher value
```

The cross-differences give the ratio.

```text
Lower : Higher
= (Higher - Mean) : (Mean - Lower)
```

### Example

Mix 30% and 50% solutions to obtain 40%.

```text
30%       40       50%
  \       |        /
   \      |       /
     10      10
```

Therefore:

```text
30% : 50%
= 10 : 10
= 1 : 1
```

Equal quantities are required.

## 6. Mixing Two Solutions

When two solutions are mixed, calculate the actual amount of solute in each solution.

### Example

20 L of 30% acid is mixed with 10 L of 60% acid.

First solution:

```text
Acid = 20 × 30/100
     = 6 L
```

Second solution:

```text
Acid = 10 × 60/100
     = 6 L
```

Total acid:

```text
6 + 6 = 12 L
```

Total solution:

```text
20 + 10 = 30 L
```

Final concentration:

```text
12/30 × 100
= 40%
```

---

## 7. Ratio of Quantities

Alligation can directly give the ratio of quantities.

### Example

Mix 20% and 60% solutions to obtain 30%.

```text
20% : 60%
= (60 - 30) : (30 - 20)
= 30 : 10
= 3 : 1
```

So:

```text
20% solution : 60% solution = 3 : 1
```

### Finding actual quantities

If total mixture = 40 L:

```text
Total ratio = 3 + 1 = 4
```

Therefore:

```text
20% solution = 40 × 3/4 = 30 L
60% solution = 40 × 1/4 = 10 L
```

## 8. Dilution

**Dilution** means reducing the concentration by adding a solvent.

For example:

```text
Acid + Water
Alcohol + Water
Salt solution + Water
```

When only water is added:

> The amount of solute remains unchanged.

### Example

30 L of 40% acid solution is diluted to 30%.

Initial acid:

```text
30 × 40/100 = 12 L
```

Final solution must contain the same 12 L acid.

Let final quantity be `V`.

```text
12/V × 100 = 30
```

Therefore:

```text
V = 40 L
```

Water added:

```text
40 - 30 = 10 L
```

---

## 9. Replacement of Mixture

A common problem pattern:

1. Start with a mixture.
2. Remove some quantity.
3. Replace it with another substance.
4. Find the final concentration.

### Example

A vessel contains 40 L milk.

10 L is removed and replaced with water.

Initially:

```text
Milk = 40 L
```

After removing 10 L milk:

```text
Milk = 30 L
```

After adding 10 L water:

```text
Total = 40 L
Milk = 30 L
```

Therefore:

```text
Milk % = 30/40 × 100
       = 75%
```

---

## 10. Repeated Replacement

Suppose:

* Total quantity = `V`
* Quantity removed each time = `x`

Fraction remaining after one operation:

```text
(V - x)/V
```

or:

```text
1 - x/V
```

After `n` operations:

```text
Original substance remaining
= Initial quantity × (1 - x/V)ⁿ
```

### Example

A 50 L vessel contains milk.

10 L is removed and replaced with water twice.

Initial milk:

```text
50 L
```

After first replacement:

```text
50 × 40/50
= 40 L
```

After second replacement:

```text
40 × 40/50
= 32 L
```

Therefore:

```text
Milk remaining = 32 L
```

Shortcut:

```text
50 × (1 - 10/50)²
= 50 × (4/5)²
= 32 L
```

## 11. Milk and Water Problems

Milk-water questions usually involve:

* Ratio
* Percentage
* Replacement
* Addition
* Removal

### Example

Milk : Water = 7 : 3.

Total mixture = 50 L.

Total ratio:

```text
7 + 3 = 10
```

Milk:

```text
50 × 7/10 = 35 L
```

Water:

```text
50 × 3/10 = 15 L
```

## 12. Mixture and Profit

Sometimes the components have different costs and the mixture is sold at a particular price.

First calculate the **cost price of the mixture** using weighted average or alligation.

### Example

10 kg material at ₹40/kg is mixed with 20 kg material at ₹50/kg.

Cost:

```text
10×40 + 20×50
= 400 + 1000
= ₹1400
```

Total quantity:

```text
30 kg
```

Cost per kg:

```text
1400/30
= ₹46.67/kg
```

If sold at ₹56/kg:

```text
Profit = 56 - 46.67
       = ₹9.33/kg
```

## 13. Adulteration

Adulteration means adding a cheaper substance to a costly substance.

Common examples:

* Water added to milk
* Cheaper material added to expensive material
* Lower-quality product mixed with higher-quality product

### Profit through adulteration

Suppose water is added to milk and water costs ₹0.

If the mixture is sold at the original price of milk, the seller gains because part of the mixture costs nothing.

### Example

10 L milk costs ₹50/L.

5 L water is added.

Total mixture:

```text
10 + 5 = 15 L
```

Cost:

```text
10 × 50 = ₹500
```

Cost per litre:

```text
500/15 = ₹33.33
```

If sold at ₹50/L:

```text
Profit per litre
= 50 - 33.33
= ₹16.67
```

## 14. Removal and Addition

When a quantity is removed from a mixture, the removed portion has the **same concentration as the current mixture**.

This is a very important assumption.

### Example

A 100 L solution is 40% acid.

10 L is removed.

Acid removed:

```text
10 × 40/100
= 4 L
```

Acid remaining:

```text
40 - 4 = 36 L
```

If 10 L water is added:

```text
Total = 100 L
Acid = 36 L
```

Final concentration:

```text
36%
```

---

## 15. Percentage Concentration

Percentage concentration is simply:

```text
Required substance / Total mixture × 100
```

### Example

A 25 L mixture contains 5 L alcohol.

```text
Alcohol %
= 5/25 × 100
= 20%
```

If 5 L water is added:

```text
New total = 30 L
Alcohol = 5 L
```

New concentration:

```text
5/30 × 100
= 16.67%
```

## 16. Equation Method

Alligation is fast, but equations are useful when the problem has several unknowns.

### Example

A 30% solution is mixed with a 50% solution to obtain 40 L of 35% solution.

Let 30% solution = `x`.

Then 50% solution:

```text
40 - x
```

Solute equation:

```text
0.30x + 0.50(40 - x) = 0.35 × 40
```

```text
0.30x + 20 - 0.50x = 14
```

```text
-0.20x = -6
```

```text
x = 30
```

Therefore:

```text
30% solution = 30 L
50% solution = 10 L
```

## 17. Common Patterns

### Pattern 1: Find concentration

```text
Solute / Total × 100
```

### Pattern 2: Find solute

```text
Total × Percentage/100
```

### Pattern 3: Find mixture ratio

Use alligation.

```text
Lower : Higher
= (Higher - Mean) : (Mean - Lower)
```

### Pattern 4: Find average price

Use weighted average.

```text
Total cost / Total quantity
```

### Pattern 5: Add water

Solute remains constant.

### Pattern 6: Remove mixture

Removed quantity contains solute according to the current concentration.

### Pattern 7: Repeated replacement

Use:

```text
Initial × (1 - x/V)ⁿ
```

---

## 18. Common Traps

### Trap 1: Taking simple average

Wrong:

```text
₹40 and ₹60 → ₹50
```

when quantities are unequal.

Use weighted average.

### Trap 2: Using wrong alligation order

Correct:

```text
Lower : Higher
= (Higher - Mean) : (Mean - Lower)
```

### Trap 3: Forgetting total quantity

If 20 L + 30 L are mixed:

```text
Total = 50 L
```

not 30 L.

### Trap 4: Treating removed liquid as pure substance

If a 40% solution is removed, the removed portion is also 40% solution.

### Trap 5: Changing solute during dilution

Adding water does not change the amount of solute.

### Trap 6: Ignoring repeated operations

If replacement happens twice, apply the remaining fraction twice.

### Trap 7: Mixing units

Do not mix:

```text
kg with grams
litres with millilitres
```

without conversion.

### Trap 8: Mean outside the two values

If two values are mixed:

```text
Lower < Mean < Higher
```

If the target lies outside this range, a normal positive mixture is impossible.

## 19. Exam Strategy

For placement aptitude:

1. Identify what is being mixed.
2. Write quantities and concentrations/prices.
3. Decide between:

   * Direct formula
   * Weighted average
   * Alligation
   * Equation method
   * Replacement formula
4. Keep units consistent.
5. For replacement, calculate the amount of solute removed.
6. Check whether the final answer lies logically between the two original values.

### Quick Decision Rule

```text
Different values + target mean
        ↓
    Alligation

Different quantities + average
        ↓
  Weighted Average

Water/solvent added
        ↓
    Solute constant

Repeated removal + replacement
        ↓
 Replacement Formula
```

### Final Memory Rule

```text
Mixture = Quantity + Value/Concentration

Weighted Average:
Total Value / Total Quantity

Alligation:
Higher - Mean : Mean - Lower

Replacement:
Initial × (1 - Removed/Total)ⁿ
```
